Someone took a photo of you  
and tried to unlock your iPhone with it.  
  
It didn’t work. Here’s the exact reason why.  
  
Face ID doesn’t use a normal camera.  
It uses an infrared dot projector.  
  
The moment you look at your phone,  
it projects 30,000 invisible dots onto your face.  
  
These dots map the exact 3D structure of your face —  
depth, curves, and distances between features.  
  
A photo is flat. It has no depth.  
  
So Face ID rejects it immediately, not because it’s “smart,”  
but because it’s measuring something a photo simply doesn’t have.  
  
That 3D map is converted into a mathematical pattern  
and stored inside the Secure Enclave —  
a chip that even Apple cannot access.  
  
Every time you unlock your phone,  
a new 3D scan is taken and compared in milliseconds.  
  
And here’s the bonus.  
  
Face ID slowly updates your face over time.  
New haircut, glasses, beard — it adapts silently
