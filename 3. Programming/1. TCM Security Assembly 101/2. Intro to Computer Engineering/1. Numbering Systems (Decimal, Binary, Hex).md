To convert **`1111` from binary to decimal**:

Each position represents a power of 2:

```
1   1   1   1
↓   ↓   ↓   ↓
2³  2²  2¹  2⁰
```

Now calculate:

(1×23)+(1×22)+(1×21)+(1×20)(1\times2^3)+(1\times2^2)+(1\times2^1)+(1\times2^0) =8+4+2+1= 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 =15=\boxed{15}

✅ **Binary `1111` = Decimal `15`**

### 1. Binary `1101` → Decimal

```
1   1   0   1
↓   ↓   ↓   ↓
2³  2²  2¹  2⁰
```

(1×8)+(1×4)+(0×2)+(1×1)(1×8)+(1×4)+(0×2)+(1×1) =8+4+0+1= 8+4+0+1 13\boxed{13}

✅ **`1101` = `13`**

### 2. Binary `1001` → Decimal

```
1   0   0   1
↓   ↓   ↓   ↓
2³  2²  2¹  2⁰
```

(1×8)+(0×4)+(0×2)+(1×1)(1×8)+(0×4)+(0×2)+(1×1) =8+0+0+1= 8+0+0+1 9\boxed{9}

✅ **`1001` = `9`**

### **[Part 3: The Binary Numbering System (Base 2)]**

The second numbering system we're going to take a look at is **binary**. If we take a look at the binary representation for 217, that is: \[\mathbf{11011001_2}\]

### **[Part 5: Bits, Bytes, Nibbles, and Converting Binary to Hex]**

Let's chat a little bit about the characteristics of binary and hexadecimal numbers and why we use hexadecimal in place of binary.

Looking back at our binary representation of 217 (\(\mathbf{1101\ 1001}\)):

- Each individual digit is called a **bit**.
- In our example, we have 8 bits, and 8 bits makes up what's called a **byte**.
- Half of a byte (4 bits) is referred to as a **nibble**.

The reason we talk about half of a byte (4 bits) and why we split it up is because **4 bits can be represented with one hex character**. That makes sense because hex is base 16, and \(2^4 = 16\). Looking at binary in 4-bit nibbles makes it very easy to convert to the hex equivalent on the fly:

- **Right nibble (\(\mathbf{1001}\))**: Positions are 8, 4, 2, 1. \(8 + 1 = 9\) (hex digit **9**).
- **Left nibble (\(\mathbf{1101}\))**: \(8 + 4 + 1 = 13\) (hex digit **D**).

Combining them gives **D9** directly. That's why you'll frequently see hexadecimal used as a replacement for binary throughout the rest of this course—it's so much easier to convert between the two, and it saves a lot of space in writing. Just think of it as an easy conversion from binary to hex.
