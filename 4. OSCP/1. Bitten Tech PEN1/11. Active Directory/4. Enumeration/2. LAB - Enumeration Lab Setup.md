This room uses a **dedicated VPN segment** — standard TryHackMe OpenVPN configs will not work.

1. Navigate to the TryHackMe Access page → select the **Breaching AD** network pack.

### 2. Configure DNS

Connecting to the VPN alone is insufficient — local DNS must resolve lab domain names.

1. Open **Advanced Network Configuration** in Kali.

![277](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020261005104322.png)

2. Double-click your connection name.

![432](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020261005104450.png)

3. Go to **IPv4 Settings**.

![459](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020261005104545.png)

4. In **Additional DNS Servers**, enter the DC IP first, then Google DNS for internet access:

```
10.200.70.101, 8.8.8.8
```

![505](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020261005104653.png)

![509](attachments/Pasted%20image%2020261005104836.png)


### **[Section 1: Transitioning from Breaching AD to AD Enumeration]**

Hello friends, in this video we move forward in our Active Directory penetration testing. We have already breached Active Directory and gained initial access to an AD machine. That initial access can occur through non-AD methods or through AD-specific methods—those are the two general approaches. We covered the AD-specific methods in the previous video where we completed the **Breaching Active Directory** room.

Now, moving forward logically, what comes after that step? Once we obtain initial, lower-level access on an AD machine, we enumerate it. Sometimes you also need to perform privilege escalation to obtain higher-level access, and even after that, the enumeration phase continues. This is because in post-exploitation, you need to move across different machines—to perform lateral movement. Reaching that stage in post-exploitation requires continuous enumeration even after privilege escalation, making it critically important. Now coming to the third room, I have opened it up here.

### **[Section 3: Privilege Escalation, Lateral Movement, and Tooling]**

Enumeration is essential for both privilege escalation and lateral movement, as it reveals what privileges we currently hold and what paths exist to escalate privileges or pivot to other machines. As shown in the diagram, once you establish proper access on a machine, you conduct internal reconnaissance and move between machines until you obtain Domain Admin or the highest administrative privilege across the entire Active Directory environment. You continue progressing, maintaining persistence, until your objective is achieved.

The primary objectives in this room involve utilizing several administrative and enumeration utilities, including the **Microsoft Management Console (MMC)**, the **Command Prompt**, **PowerShell**, and finally **BloodHound**, which is the most critical tool for Active Directory enumeration.

### **[Section 6: Shared Network Constraints, Reset Voting, and Platform Comparison]**

The network repeatedly drops with `DNS name not found`, forcing me to restart the VPN and attempt resetting the network. The fundamental limitation is that you do not receive a dedicated network instance; you are placed on a shared network segment.

Resetting the network requires a group vote—it only resets after accumulating five votes. Because multiple users work on the same machine simultaneously, their conflicting actions can disable the network or trigger errors that stall your work. I am pointing this out so you are prepared for these hurdles when attempting this lab.

We have to wait for the network to reset and restore availability, which happens unpredictably. Even on the TryHackMe forums, many users report that this specific room is unstable. In comparison, dedicated lab environments like **Hack The Box Pro Labs** (such as Dante or RastaLabs) are significantly better regarding stability and performance.
