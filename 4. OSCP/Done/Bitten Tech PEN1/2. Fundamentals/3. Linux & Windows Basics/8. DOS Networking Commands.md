# DOS Networking Commands (Windows CMD)

> Essential networking commands in Windows CMD for diagnostics, troubleshooting, and reconnaissance. These are heavily used in penetration testing.

---

## `ping` — Test Network Connectivity

Sends **ICMP echo requests** to a target and checks for a response. Verifies whether a host is online and reachable.

### Basic Ping (4 packets)

```
C:\>ping www.google.com

Pinging www.google.com [2001:4860:482a:7700::] with 32 bytes of data:
Reply from 2001:4860:482a:7700::: time=7ms
Reply from 2001:4860:482a:7700::: time=7ms
Reply from 2001:4860:482a:7700::: time=12ms

Ping statistics for 2001:4860:482a:7700:::
    Packets: Sent = 3, Received = 3, Lost = 0 (0% loss),
Approximate round trip times in milli-seconds:
    Minimum = 7ms, Maximum = 12ms, Average = 8ms
```

- By default, sends **4 ICMP packets**
- If the target is a hostname, it **resolves it to an IP** first
- Shows: reply bytes, round-trip **time**, and **TTL** (Time to Live)
- TTL value differs per OS — useful for OS fingerprinting

### Continuous Ping (`-t`)

```
C:\>ping -t www.google.com
Reply from 2001:4860:482a:7700::: time=7ms
Reply from 2001:4860:482a:7700::: time=8ms
...
^C
```

Pings **indefinitely** until you press `Ctrl+C`.

### Stopping Running Commands

| Shortcut | Action |
| -------- | ------ |
| `Ctrl+C` | **Terminate** the current foreground process (cannot resume) |
| `Ctrl+Z` | Suspend/sleep the process (can resume in some cases) |

## `ipconfig` — Network Configuration

Shows IP addresses, subnet masks, and gateways for all network adapters.

### Basic Usage

```
C:\>ipconfig

Wireless LAN adapter Wi-Fi:
   IPv4 Address. . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.3
   Subnet Mask . . . . . . . . . . . : 255.255.255.0
   Default Gateway . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1
```

### Detailed Info (`/all`)

```
C:\>ipconfig /all
   Host Name . . . . . . . . . . . . : DESKTOP-2ER5CUI
   Description . . . . . . . . . . . : Intel(R) Wi-Fi 6E AX211 160MHz
   Physical Address. . . . . . . . . : E4-0D-36-8A-BA-A6    ← MAC address
   DHCP Enabled. . . . . . . . . . . : Yes
   IPv4 Address. . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.3
   Default Gateway . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1
   DHCP Server . . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1
   DNS Servers . . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1
   Lease Obtained. . . . . . . . . . : Friday, 14 August, 2026 09:01:09 PM
   Lease Expires . . . . . . . . . . : Sunday, 16 August, 2026 01:11:17 PM
```

Shows: hostname, MAC address, DHCP status, DNS servers, lease times, and adapter chipset info for **every** adapter.

### Key `ipconfig` Switches

| Switch | Description |
| ------ | ----------- |
| `/all` | Full configuration (MAC, DHCP, DNS, etc.) |
| `/release` | Release current IPv4 address |
| `/renew` | Request new IPv4 address from DHCP |
| `/flushdns` | **Purge the DNS resolver cache** |
| `/displaydns` | Display contents of the DNS cache |
| `/registerdns` | Refresh all DHCP leases and re-register DNS |

> **Important:** If DNS resolution is stale or cached incorrectly, run `ipconfig /flushdns` to clear the cache.

## `arp -a` — ARP Table (IP ↔ MAC Mapping)

Displays the **ARP cache** — the mapping between IP addresses and MAC (physical) addresses on your network.

```
C:\>arp -a

Interface: 192.168.1.3 --- 0x4
  Internet Address      Physical Address      Type
  192.168.1.1           20-0c-86-d7-0b-88     dynamic
  192.168.1.2           da-01-ab-d4-74-9c     dynamic
  192.168.1.255         ff-ff-ff-ff-ff-ff     static
  224.0.0.22            01-00-5e-00-00-16     static
```

- **Dynamic** entries = learned through network communication
- **Static** entries = predefined (broadcast, multicast addresses)
- ARP's job: connect **IP to MAC** addresses

> **Security use:** If you know an IP on your network, ARP can reveal its MAC address.

## `getmac` — Display All MAC Addresses

```
C:\>getmac

Physical Address    Transport Name
=================== ==========================================================
00-50-56-C0-00-01   \Device\Tcpip_{79FCB40F-C28A-4CA3-947F-978FBF896C16}
E4-0D-36-8A-BA-A6   \Device\Tcpip_{1A41C95E-0575-4FEF-A3D7-0C42E99007E8}
E4-0D-36-8A-BA-AA   Media disconnected
```

Shows MAC addresses for **all** adapters (physical and virtual). Disconnected ones show "Media disconnected."

> **Alternative:** `ipconfig /all` also shows MAC addresses (as "Physical Address").

## `tracert` — Trace Route to Destination

Traces the **path** (all intermediate hops/routers) between your system and a destination.

```
C:\>tracert www.google.com

Tracing route to www.google.com [2001:4860:482b:7700::]
over a maximum of 30 hops:

  1     2 ms     2 ms    11 ms  2401:4900:88eb:7076:220c:86ff:fed7:b88
  2     *        *        *     Request timed out.
  3     7 ms     5 ms     6 ms  2404:a800:1a00:209::279
  4     7 ms     8 ms     7 ms  2001:4860:482b:7700::

Trace complete.
```

- Sends **3 ICMP packets** at each hop
- Shows the **IP address** and **round-trip time** for each intermediate router
- `*` = request timed out (router didn't respond — may be configured to block ICMP)

### When to Use

- **Network diagnostics:** Find where a connection drops or slows down
- **Security/recon:** See what routers sit between you and a target
- **Troubleshooting:** Identify which hop is causing latency or packet loss

## `netstat` — Network Statistics & Connections

Shows active TCP/UDP connections, listening ports, and which processes are communicating with external systems.

### Basic Usage

```
C:\>netstat

Active Connections

  Proto  Local Address          Foreign Address        State
  TCP    192.168.1.3:9356       34.93.39.2:https       ESTABLISHED
  TCP    192.168.1.3:10692      a23-46-207-8:https     ESTABLISHED
```

### Numeric Addresses (`-n`)

```
C:\>netstat -n

  Proto  Local Address          Foreign Address        State
  TCP    192.168.1.3:9356       34.93.39.2:443         ESTABLISHED
  TCP    192.168.1.3:10692      23.46.207.8:443        ESTABLISHED
```

Shows raw IP addresses and port numbers instead of resolved hostnames.

### With Process ID (`-on`)

```
C:\>netstat -on

  Proto  Local Address          Foreign Address        State           PID
  TCP    192.168.1.3:9356       34.93.39.2:443         ESTABLISHED     18784
  TCP    192.168.1.3:29639      35.241.29.71:443       ESTABLISHED     18784
```

> **Security use:** If a process is communicating suspiciously (malware, unauthorized connections), get its **PID** and terminate it with `taskkill /PID <number>`.

### Key `netstat` Switches

| Switch | Description |
| ------ | ----------- |
| `-a` | Display **all** connections and listening ports |
| `-b` | Show the **executable** creating each connection |
| `-n` | Display addresses and ports in **numerical** form |
| `-o` | Show the **owning Process ID (PID)** |
| `-p <proto>` | Filter by protocol: TCP, UDP, TCPv6, UDPv6 |
| `-r` | Display the **routing table** |
| `-s` | Per-protocol **statistics** |
| `-f` | Show **Fully Qualified Domain Names** (FQDN) |

> **Combine switches:** `netstat -ano` shows all connections with numeric IPs and PIDs — the most common combo for security analysis.

## `telnet` — Remote Login Protocol

```
C:\>telnet smtp.gmail.com 587
```

> [!WARNING]
> Telnet is **disabled by default** in Windows. To enable it:
> 1. Search → "Turn Windows features on or off"
> 2. Check **Telnet Client**
> 3. Click OK and wait for installation

- Used for remote login to systems
- Sends data in **plaintext** (insecure — prefer SSH instead)
- Syntax: `telnet <host> <port>`

---

## `ftp` — File Transfer Protocol

```
C:\>ftp 192.168.1.1
> Connected
Username: admin
Password: ****
ftp> ls
```

- Available **by default** in Windows (no enabling needed)
- Syntax: `ftp <IP address or hostname>`
- After connecting, prompts for username and password
- Use `ls` to list files, `get` to download, `put` to upload

## Quick Reference Summary

| Task | Command |
| ---- | ------- |
| Test connectivity | `ping <host>` |
| Continuous ping | `ping -t <host>` |
| View IP config | `ipconfig` |
| Full network details + MAC | `ipconfig /all` |
| Flush DNS cache | `ipconfig /flushdns` |
| Release/renew IP | `ipconfig /release` → `ipconfig /renew` |
| View ARP table | `arp -a` |
| Get MAC addresses | `getmac` |
| Trace route | `tracert <host>` |
| View active connections | `netstat` |
| Connections with PIDs | `netstat -ano` |
| Remote login | `telnet <host> <port>` |
| File transfer | `ftp <host>` |
| Stop running command | `Ctrl+C` |
