# Schedule

> A **weekend-based, recorded-lecture** format designed for flexibility, self-paced learning, and consistency over **4–5 months**.

---
## Weekly Lecture Schedule

| Day          | Event                        |
| ------------ | ---------------------------- |
| **Friday**   | Lecture uploaded at 8:00 PM  |
| **Saturday** | Lecture uploaded at 8:00 PM  |
| **Sunday**   | Catch-up / Live sessions     |

- Friday's lecture → watch on Saturday; Saturday's lecture → watch on Sunday.
- Weekdays are available for finishing anything left over.
- **Live sessions** may occasionally be scheduled on Sundays — notifications and links are shared in the **Telegram group** in advance.
- All sessions are in **English**.

> [!NOTE]
> **First week only:** lectures release on Saturday and Sunday. From week 2 onward, the regular Friday–Saturday schedule begins.

---
## Benefits of Recorded Lectures

### 1. Flexibility
- Watch at **any time** according to your own schedule — useful for students with exams or working professionals with varying routines.
- **Pause anytime** — critical when debugging code as a beginner, since fixing errors can take time and you need the ability to stop and work through them.

### 2. Time Savings
- Watch at **1.5× or 2× speed** — especially helpful for working professionals or students who already know a topic (e.g., Python).
- **Re-watch** lectures as many times as needed for revision.

## 30-Day Consistency Challenge

Commit **2–3 hours daily** to AI/ML preparation starting from Day 1.

### What to do in those 2–3 hours
- Learn new concepts
- Practice coding and solve assignment problems
- Build projects
- Revise previously learned concepts

### How to start
1. Take an index / calendar and **mark Days 1–30**.
2. Plan which 2–3 hour block works for you (morning, evening, or night).
3. Stick to it daily — consistency in the initial days builds momentum for harder topics later.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Understand logic, don't memorize.** This is career preparation, not semester-exam cramming.
> - If you understand the logic, assignment problems become enjoyable and interviews feel confident.
> - Syntax may be forgotten over time, but **logic stays for a lifetime** — revisiting a concept later takes far less time than learning it the first time.

> [!TIP]
> Write as much code as possible. Practice builds the logical thinking that will help most in the long term. After 30 days of consistent effort, the difference will be clearly visible.
