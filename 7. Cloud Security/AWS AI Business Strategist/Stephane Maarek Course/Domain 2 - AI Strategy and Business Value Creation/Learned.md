# Build vs Buy vs Partner

> A use case says **what** to get. How to get it is a second decision — ask where the advantage lives.

---

## The Three Paths

| Path | What You Do | What You Own | Timeline | Best Fit |
|------|-------------|-------------|----------|----------|
| **Build** | Train or assemble your own model on your own data | The model, the pipeline, the know-how | Months | The advantage is in **your own data or logic** |
| **Buy** | Subscribe to a service that already does it | A subscription and your configuration | Days to weeks | You need speed and the capability **does not set you apart** |
| **Partner** | Bring in a firm with the skills or the domain | The outcome, delivered with someone else's expertise | Contract + scope | You **lack expertise** you cannot acquire in time |

### CozyCo Examples

- **Build** → Demand forecast on 6 years of its own sales data
- **Buy** → Support assistant for policy questions
- **Partner** → Damage assessment on returns (nobody in-house, 4-month window)

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> The answer follows the **use case**, not a company-wide policy — the same company builds, buys, and partners on different initiatives.

---

## Five Constraints That Can Change the Answer

The advantage question gives a default. Five practical constraints can override it:

| Constraint | Effect |
|------------|--------|
| **Budget** | Building costs more upfront than buying |
| **Timeline** | A fixed date rules out a build that needs months |
| **Internal Capabilities** | No data scientists means no build, whatever the data |
| **Vendor Proposals** | A strong offer on the market makes buying the rational default |
| **Regulatory Compliance** | A regulated domain can push toward a partner who knows the rules |

- Missing skills change the **path**, never the decision to proceed.

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> "We lack the skills, so we **delay** until we have hired" and "we lack the skills, so we **terminate**" are both distractors — missing skills mean **buy or partner**.
