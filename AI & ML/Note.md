**Which Jobs are at Risk?** The speaker argues that any job that does not require active "thinking" or involves performing the same repetitive tasks daily is at risk of being automated.

- **The "Repetition" Warning:** A former manager at Sandvine once advised that if you go to the office every day knowing exactly what needs to be done and how to do it, you are in a precarious position.
- **Automation of Simple Tasks:** Repetitive tasks in service-based roles—such as clicking the same buttons or performing routine front-end adjustments like changing a button's color or shape—are already effectively automated or "gone".

**The Value of Critical Thinking and Creativity** The speaker emphasizes that AI's primary limitation is its inability to "think" independently; rather, it functions as a massive database.

- **AI vs. Human Creativity:** AI can paint a horse very well because it has a database of existing horse paintings. However, if no one had ever painted a dragon, the AI would not know how to start, whereas a human can "think" and imagine how to create one.
- **Safe Jobs:** Your job is considered safe if you go to work without a clear "clue" as to exactly what challenges you will face or how you will solve them, as this requires the problem-solving skills that AI lacks.

**The Reality of the Job Market** Despite the marketing claims from AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude that they are "ending" front-end or back-end development, the speaker points out a significant irony: these very companies continue to list numerous openings for front-end and back-end engineers on their own career sites. The core message is that **jobs are not disappearing**, but the demand is shifting toward those who can **increase their capacity to think** rather than just execute routine code.
