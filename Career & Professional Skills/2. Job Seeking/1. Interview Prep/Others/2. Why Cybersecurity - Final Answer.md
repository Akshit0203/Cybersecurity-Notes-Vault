# Why Cybersecurity? — Final Answer

> [!summary] TL;DR
> - **Target length:** 60–90 seconds (~200–230 words). Your existing "2-minute version" is closer to 3 minutes spoken — too long for an opener.
> - **Structure:** Curiosity → Deliberate choice → Proof → Why it still excites me → Why this role.
> - **Goal:** Show it's a *genuine, long-term* interest and that you've *tested* it in real environments. Then stop and let them ask follow-ups.

## 1. The Answer (say this)

> [!quote] ~75–90 seconds
> Honestly, it started as curiosity long before I knew the word "cybersecurity." As a kid, I wasn't satisfied just playing games — I wanted to know how people were able to change them. When I got my first Android phone, the first thing I did was root it… and I bricked it. That was my first real lesson: you can't safely change a system you don't understand.
>
> During the COVID lockdown that curiosity got serious. I went deep into reverse engineering, networking and how client-server systems actually work. Building my own labs pushed me into cloud — I set up my own infrastructure on Azure — and later into hardware and wireless security with tools like the HackRF and Flipper Zero.
>
> In college I deliberately tried software engineering, cloud, DevOps and AI, but security was the one I kept coming back to on evenings and weekends without anyone asking me to. So I decided to make it my profession. I built my fundamentals properly — Security+, CCNA, AWS — and then tested myself in real environments: a security startup, a specialised Indian Army internship, CloudSEK, and the Gurugram Police cyber internship, where I finished 3rd out of more than 15,000 applicants.
>
> What keeps me here is that security pulls together everything I enjoy — systems, networks, cloud and problem-solving — and there's something new to learn every single day. That's exactly why I'm excited about **[this role / company]**.

## 2. Short Version (~30 seconds)

Use when the interviewer is clearly short on time, or it's a quick HR screen.

> [!quote]
> It started with curiosity — as a kid I wanted to know how games and phones could be modified, not just use them. That turned into reverse engineering, networking, cloud and hardware security. In college I tried other fields, but security was the one I kept doing in my free time, so I made it my career. Since then I've worked with a security startup, the Indian Army, CloudSEK and Gurugram Police — where I finished 3rd of 15,000+ applicants — and every one of them made me more sure this is what I want to do.

## 3. Structure (memorise this, not the words)

If you blank out, just walk through these five beats in your own words:

1. **Spark** — Curious kid → wanted to know *how* games/phones could be changed. Bricked my phone → "understand before you change."
2. **Deepening** — COVID: reverse engineering, networking → labs → own cloud (Azure) → hardware/wireless (HackRF, Flipper Zero).
3. **Deliberate choice** — Tried SWE, cloud, DevOps, AI. Security is what I did *without being asked*. Passion → profession.
4. **Proof** — Fundamentals (Security+, CCNA, AWS) → startup → Indian Army → CloudSEK → Gurugram Police (**3rd / 15,000+**).
5. **Why still + why here** — Combines everything I enjoy, always something new → why this role.

## 5. Things to NEVER say in an interview

> [!danger] Keep these out — they come from the informal notes
> - **Phishing friends' game accounts (zphisher)** — this is unauthorised access to other people's accounts. Even as a teenager, it raises an ethics red flag. Don't mention it.
> - **RATs (CraxsRAT, XWorm), C2 servers** — even though it was on your own devices, it sounds like malware use out of context. If you want to mention it, frame it only as: *"I tested malware behaviour in my own lab on my own devices to understand how attackers get persistence."* — and only if asked about labs.
> - **Using cheats / hacked APKs to beat friends, DDoS-ing game servers** — say "I was curious how games could be modified," not "I wanted to be a hacker / cheat."
> - **Card / metro card cloning attempts** — say "I researched why payment and metro cards resist cloning — tokenisation and cryptography." Don't say you tried to use a cloned card on a POS.
> - **Army details** — don't say what was "deployed on the border" or anything operational. "I learned how secure military communication systems are designed" is enough.

> [!warning] Other pitfalls
> - **Don't list every certification.** Name 2–3 at most in the answer; the rest are on your CV.
> - **Don't over-tell the childhood part.** It's the hook, not the story. 2–3 sentences max.
> - **Don't say "I've wanted to be a hacker since I was 4."** Say "curious about how things work."
> - **Don't end with "there's no looking back."** End by connecting to *their* role.
> - **Don't memorise word-for-word.** Memorise the 5 beats (Section 3) — it'll sound natural instead of rehearsed.
