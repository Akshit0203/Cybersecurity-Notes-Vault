# Formal (Shorter)

## Why Cybersecurity? (2-Minute Version)

For me, cybersecurity wasn't a career decision—it has been a pattern throughout my entire life.

I got my first Windows PC when I was around four years old. Like most kids, I enjoyed playing games, but what fascinated me wasn't just playing them—it was discovering that people could actually change how they worked. I found modified games, cheat codes, and other things that made me wonder, _"How is this even possible?"_ That curiosity eventually became an interest in understanding software rather than simply using it.

A few years later, when I got my first Android phone, the first thing I wanted to do wasn't install social media—it was root the phone because I wanted to understand what having complete control over a device actually meant. I even managed to brick the phone and had to get it repaired, but that experience taught me one of my earliest lessons: if you don't understand a system, experimenting blindly can break it.

During the COVID lockdown, that curiosity grew much deeper. I started learning about reverse engineering, networking, operating systems, and software internals. As I built my own lab environments, I naturally moved into cloud computing because I needed to build and manage my own infrastructure. Later, I became interested in hardware security as well, working with devices like the Flipper Zero, HackRF, Raspberry Pi, and SDRs to understand wireless communication and embedded systems. At that point, I realized cybersecurity wasn't just about software—it brought together networking, cloud, operating systems, hardware, and engineering.

Eventually I decided to turn that passion into a profession. I earned certifications like CEH, Security+, CCNA, Network+, AWS Solutions Architect, and CHFI, but more importantly, I wanted real-world experience. That led me to internships where I worked on vulnerability assessments, cloud security, enterprise infrastructure, and digital forensics.

As I became more confident in my skills, I deliberately started applying to highly competitive national-level opportunities. I was selected for programs with the Indian Army, CloudSEK, and the Gurugram Police Cyber Security Internship. Those experiences gave me three very different perspectives on cybersecurity—military communications, enterprise product security, and cybercrime investigations. Finishing **3rd among more than 15,000 applicants** in the Gurugram Police program gave me confidence that I could perform in highly competitive environments.

When I look back now, I don't think there was ever a single moment where I chose cybersecurity. Every stage of my life naturally pushed me deeper into it. What started as curiosity about how games worked eventually became a passion for building secure systems and solving engineering problems that protect people at scale.

That's why Security Engineering is exactly where I see myself, and that's why I'm excited about the opportunity to work at Google.

When a problem looks impossible, don't try to solve everything at once.

Break it into smaller problems, solve each one methodically, and the larger problem becomes manageable.

That mindset has stayed with me ever since.

I enjoy solving engineering problems.

And I enjoy working on problems that have real impact.

When I look back now, I don't think there was ever one moment where I chose cybersecurity.
