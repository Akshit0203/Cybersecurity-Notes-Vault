## Resume Formatting Checklist

- Fix formatting of all sections, especially the skills section.
- Use a cleaner resume format with better spacing, alignment, and section hierarchy.
- Make your resume in LaTeX and keep it A4 size only.
- Move the Education section upwards, just below Profile Summary.
- Add a Projects section and include 2 good projects relevant to work.
- Reduce the number of bullet points in work experience. Around 3 concise impact-focused points per organization is enough.
- Convert long paragraphs into shorter readable bullet points.
- Add your GitHub, TryHackMe, and LinkedIn profile links at the top near contact details.

---

## Quantifying Your Work on a CV

- CV should also tell the size of the project.
- Use action verbs: **Build, Deployed, Developed**.
- Example: *"I developed 39 workflows, used by 50k users spanning 17 countries."*
- Quantifying work is very important.
- Show improved metrics — e.g., *"Improved avg time spent by avg 4 minutes."*
- Size and impact of work should be displayed.
- **Cover letter:** Make it according to the company as well (paste the job description).

## Top Hiring Advice from Google HR

### 1. Use the XYZ Resume Formula

#### Formula

**Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]**

#### Example

> "Increased lead generation by 25% by launching a Meta lookalike campaign."

Stating that an activity took place and then a result was obtained is not enough. I need to know how you accomplished it. Not just that it happened.

Let's look at the first bullet. What did you do to increase the accuracy? What did you do to enhance performance? How do you know it was state of the art? See where I'm going with this?

This means that you want to focus on accomplishments — quantitative results and the impact that you had as a result. It's probably easiest to explain this by using a few examples from the Google recruiters' YouTube videos themselves.

For example, imagine an applicant who wants to make clear that he or she is a member of a prestigious group. Here's the OK way, the better way, and the best way to describe this on a resume, according to Google:

- **OK:** "Member of Leadership for Tomorrow Society"
- **Better:** "Selected as one of 275 for this 12-month professional development program for high-achieving diverse talent."
- **Best:** "Selected as one of 275 participants nationwide for this 12-month professional development program for high-achieving diverse talent based on leadership potential and academic success."

Here's another example, this one for a technical position in which the applicant wants to point out that he or she placed second in a hackathon.

- **OK:** "Won second place in hackathon."
- **Better:** "Won second place out of 50 teams in hackathon."
- **Best:** "Won second place out of 50 teams in hackathon at NJ Tech by working with two colleagues to develop an app that synchronizes mobile calendars."

(In this case, the last, "best" is my own interpretation; Google doesn't actually provide the third suggestion. But I hope the point is clear.)

Here's a final example, intended for a business applicant who wants to show how much he or she contributed in a client support role:

- **OK:** "Grew revenue for small and medium business clients."
- **Better:** "Grew revenue for small and medium business clients by 10% QoQ"
- **Best:** "Grew revenue for 15 small and medium business clients by 10% QoQ by mapping new software features as solutions to their business goals."

> **Note on jargon:** Use shorthand like "QoQ" (for quarter over quarter) only if you're 100 percent sure that the resume reviewer will know exactly what you mean.

#### Breaking Down the XYZ Formula for Cybersecurity

- **X (Achievement):** The specific goal reached, vulnerability resolved, or security incident mitigated.
- **Y (Measurement):** The quantifiable metric, dollar amount, percentage of risk reduced, or time saved.
- **Z (Action):** The specific skill, tool, framework, or methodology used.

#### Cybersecurity Examples Using the XYZ Formula

Use the following examples as inspiration to draft impactful bullet points for your own experience:

**Security Operations Center (SOC) / Incident Response**

> "Reduced critical incident response time by **40%** (Y) by deploying automated SIEM alerts and implementing new threat detection playbooks (Z), resulting in zero breaches during high-alert periods (X)."

**Vulnerability Management / Penetration Testing**

> "Identified and patched **250+** critical software vulnerabilities (Y) by conducting routine OWASP-based penetration testing and utilizing vulnerability scanners (Z), improving the overall network security score by **35%** (X)."

**Identity and Access Management (IAM) / Compliance**

> "Decreased unauthorized access attempts by **65%** (Y) by upgrading access controls, enforcing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), and conducting employee phishing awareness training (Z), achieving strict ISO 27001 compliance (X)."

**Cloud Security Engineering**

> "Achieved **99.9%** cloud infrastructure uptime with zero unauthorized configuration changes (X) as measured by quarterly security audits (Y), by deploying automated identity management and implementing CIS AWS Benchmarks (Z)."

#### 3 Pro-Tips for a Tech Resume

1. **Start with action verbs:** Always lead your bullet points with words like _Mitigated, Designed, Automated, Engineered, Spearheaded,_ or _Resolved_.
2. **Highlight your tech stack:** Naturally integrate the exact tools (e.g., Splunk, CrowdStrike, AWS, Python, Wireshark) and frameworks (e.g., MITRE ATT&CK, NIST CSF) directly into the "Z" portion of your formula.
3. **Use real-world labs if entry-level:** If you are early in your career, apply the XYZ formula to capture-the-flag (CTF) events or hands-on home lab projects (e.g., _"Configured a honeynet environment (Z) that logged and analyzed 50+ attempted network attacks (Y), improving threat intelligence gathering skills (X)"_).

#### Why It Works

- Shows measurable impact
- Makes achievements clear
- Helps recruiters quickly understand value

#### Action Steps

- Add numbers and metrics
- Focus on outcomes, not just tasks
- Quantify projects, internships, and leadership work

### 2. Treat the Recruiter Call Like a Real Interview

#### Important Reality

The recruiter screening round is not just a casual conversation.

#### What Recruiters Evaluate

- Communication skills
- Cultural fit
- Resume consistency
- Interest in the role
- Confidence and clarity

#### Key Lesson

A bad recruiter call can stop the process before technical rounds begin.

#### Action Steps

- Prepare self-introduction
- Know your resume deeply
- Research the role and team
- Speak clearly and confidently

### 3. Customize Your Resume for Every Role

#### Reality

Recruiters spend only a few seconds scanning resumes.

#### Main Mistake

Using the same generic resume everywhere.

#### Why Candidates Fail

- Resume doesn't match job description
- Important keywords missing
- Skills not aligned with role requirements

#### Action Steps

- Tailor resume for each application
- Match keywords from JD
- Highlight relevant projects and skills first
- Remove unrelated clutter

#### Goal

Increase ATS and recruiter shortlisting chances.

### 4. Referrals Matter a Lot

#### Key Insight

Referred candidates have much higher chances of getting interviews.

#### Why Referrals Work

- Recruiters trust internal recommendations
- Applications get faster visibility
- Huge applicant pools make referrals powerful

#### Action Steps

- Network on LinkedIn
- Connect with employees professionally
- Build genuine relationships
- Ask for referrals only after meaningful interaction

#### Important

A referral helps you get noticed — it does not guarantee selection.

### 6. Show Your Thinking Process

#### Important Insight

Google evaluates your approach, not just the final answer.

#### What Interviewers Want

- Logical thinking
- Communication clarity
- Optimization mindset
- Problem-solving strategy

#### Main Mistake

Staying silent while solving problems.

#### Action Steps

- Think out loud
- Explain assumptions

#### Goal

Make your reasoning visible.

### 7. Hiring Committee Makes the Final Decision

#### Important Reality

Your interviewer alone does not decide your selection.

#### What Happens

A separate hiring committee reviews:

- Resume
- Recruiter feedback
- Interview scores

#### Why Google Uses This

To reduce bias and standardize hiring quality.

#### Key Lesson

Consistency across all rounds matters more than one amazing round.

## Education vs. Experience Ordering

- **Students / recent graduates:** Education before experience.
- **Experienced professionals:** Experience before education (in reverse chronological order), if you've been in the workforce for more than a position or two.

For recent graduates, Google insists on: school, degree, major, GPA, and month and year of graduation. But the further away you are from college, the less college and university information you're expected to include.

> "Generally the more recently you attended university, the more detail you should include here." — Jeremy Ong, Google Technical Recruiter

The one caveat here, and frankly it's not something that Google addresses, is how to handle year of graduation if you're an older applicant.

There's a point at which Google probably doesn't want to know your age, so as to reduce the odds of ever having to defend against an age discrimination claim. Obviously putting on your resume that you graduated from college in 1990 suggests you're well over 40.

Beyond that, however, focus on things that are most relevant for the position, and don't be afraid to cut older positions and achievements, especially if they're not relevant.

## Resume Optimization — The STAR-T Method

- **The Problem:** Vague resume bullets (e.g., "Proficient in Wireshark") only tell a manager you have heard of a tool, not that you can use it.
- **The Fix (STAR-T Framework):** Rewrite bullets to include at least 3 of these 5 elements:
    - **S** — Situation
    - **T** — Task
    - **A** — Action
    - **R** — Result
    - **T** — Tool
- **Comparison Example:**
    - **Before:** "Set up a home lab"
    - **After:** "Built a 4-VM home lab (Kali, Windows Server, Ubuntu, Metasploitable) to simulate phishing, privilege escalation, and lateral movement. Documented findings in structured incident reports with IOCs and remediation steps."
- **Hiring Manager Insight:** Managers only spend 30–45 seconds on a resume; every bullet must prove you can do the job.

## What Hiring Managers Actually Look for in Freshers

### 1. A Nice, Simple, Clean Professional CV

Your CV should be so simple and clean that the hiring person can understand in one glance:

- What you've done
- Your skillset
- Your achievements

Remember, HRs receive hundreds of CVs every day. If your resume is too fancy or complicated, they are not going to spend extra time understanding it.

And yes… don't lie on your CV. Interviewers can usually identify that from your answers.

### 2. Understand the Basics

A lot of beginners jump directly into advanced concepts while skipping fundamentals. Build a strong foundation.

If you are a fresher, nobody is going to ask you how to reverse engineer malware. They will ask:

- Basic port scanning
- Some Nmap flags
- Basic networking
- Firmware basics
- Tools and their alternatives
- Your approach toward solving problems

If you are applying for a defensive role, they may ask:

- How EDR works
- How logs are generated
- How alerts trigger
- Important Event IDs
- False positives vs true positives

The questions can be anything, but mostly they revolve around these areas. If you are applying for a fresher or internship role, a smart interviewer should ask these kinds of questions.

### 3. Social Proof Matters

Just like candidates research companies before interviews, interviewers also research candidates.

At least I used to do that.

If someone mentioned GitHub, LinkedIn, or project links in their resume, I used to check them properly. Then I would ask questions around those projects.

This helps us understand:

- Did the candidate actually build the project
- Or was it just copied
- And how deeply they understand their own work

So start building your presence on professional platforms like LinkedIn.

### 4. Certifications

This might be a little controversial, but certifications do help during the screening round. A good certification from a credible and recognized institute gives you leverage over the crowd.

And no, I'm not talking about CEH certifications that everybody already has. You already know which certifications are actually respected in the industry.

### 5. Skills

You can have everything:

- Certificates
- LinkedIn
- Projects
- Resume

But if you don't have actual skills, then it's just daydreaming.

### 6. Communication

Last but not least — communication. You should know how to explain:

- Who you are
- What you know
- And what you can do

…in a way the other person can actually understand.

Because even if you have great skills, if you cannot communicate them properly in an interview, then there's no point. Technical knowledge is important but communicating that knowledge is equally important.

## Structural & Formatting Standards

- **Maintain One Page:** For internship applications, there is no reason to exceed a single page. Multiple pages are reserved for candidates with significant experience (7–9+ years).
- **Consistency is Mandatory:** A common critique was the mixing of formats (paragraphs, bullet points, and tables). Stick to one consistent style. Tables, in particular, should be avoided as they can cause issues with Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) and look cluttered.
- **Remove Irrelevant Education:** Do not include 10th or 12th-grade marks. Recruiters only care about current university-level achievements.
- **Professional Polish:** Spelling and grammar are critical. A misspelling of _Burp Suite_ was specifically pointed out as a red flag. Use tools like _ChatGPT_ to check for errors and platforms like _Novoresume_ for professional templates.

---

## Optimizing Resume Sections

- **Experience & Impact:** Stop listing generic responsibilities. Instead of saying you "gained hands-on experience," describe the specific technical work you performed and, crucially, the **measurable impact**. For instance, a resume was commended that detailed a specific integration which led to a "25% increase in operational efficiency" .
- **Projects over Skills:** Move technical skills to the bottom of the resume. Because skills are often just keywords, it is better to elevate _Projects_ and _Professional Experience_ to the top where recruiters can see your practical application of tools .
- **Refining Skills:** Avoid including subjective soft skills like "teamwork" or "oral communication," as these are expected. Focus strictly on technical proficiencies — tools, languages, and frameworks .
- **Achievements:** Distinguish between learning and achievement. Solving 100+ _DSA_ (Data Structures and Algorithms) questions is a study habit, not an achievement. True achievements include winning hackathons, placing in _CTF_ (Capture The Flag) competitions, or receiving industry-recognized certifications .

---

## Advice on Narrative & Detail

- **Show, Don't Just Tell:** Avoid "fluff" phrases like "specializing in corporate-level data integration." Be concrete: What specific tools did you use? What was the result?.
- **Quantify Contributions:** Whether it is a project or an internship, always include the "How" and the "Why." If you analyzed a dataset, mention the library used (e.g., _Python_) and the outcome (e.g., "reduced downtime by 15%").
