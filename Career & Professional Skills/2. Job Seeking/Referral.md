
How NOT to Ask for an Internship/Job Referral (and How to Actually Get One)  
  
1. The Resume Dump  
"Hi, here’s my resume. Can you refer me to a suitable job. Thanks!" No introduction. No context. Just a cold PDF slapped into my inbox like a ransom note.  
  
2. The Desperate  
"URGENT: Need internship in 2 weeks or I fail. Can you hire me?" Nothing says "I’m a great candidate" like panic and poor planning. Bonus negative points if the resume still has "References available upon request."  
  
3. The LinkedIn Stranger  
"Hi , we’re connected. You work at Microsoft. Need job ASAP. Can you refer me?" Ah yes, because that one time I clicked "Accept" on your connection request totally means I owe you a favor.  
  
4. The Generic Beg  
"Dear Sir/Madam, I am a hardworking student seeking internship..."  
If your message sounds like it was written by a 1990s fax machine, you’re doing it wrong. And no, "Sir/Madam," this isn’t a formal letter to the Queen.  
  
Why These Approaches Fail  
* You’re a stranger asking for a favor with zero effort.  
* People refer candidates they trust—not random PDFs.  
* Lazy outreach = lazy work ethic (or so it seems).  
  
How to Do It Right.  
1. Start With a Human Connection. "Hi , I admired your work on [specific project]. I’m studying [field] and would love to learn about your career path."  
  
2. Offer Value First. Engage with their content, share useful resources, or offer to help with something small.  
  
3. Ask Smart, Not Hard. "I’m applying to [Company] and noticed you worked there. Any advice on what they look for in interns?"  
  
The Golden Rule - The best requests come from people who:  
* Show genuine curiosity (not desperation).  
* Have done their homework (no generic messages).  
* Build relationships before they need something.  
  
Before hitting send, ask: "If I received this message, would I want to help?" If not, then rewrite it.
