
## Day 1 at a New Job

> Take notes — you won't remember everything.

### Understand the Basics

- What exactly is your role beyond the job description?
- What does your team actually work on day-to-day?
- What tools, systems, and processes are used?

### People First

- Meet your manager, align on expectations early
- Understand how they communicate (Slack, calls, email?)
- Introduce yourself to key team members
- Know who to go to for what

### Clarity > Confidence

- What are your first week priorities?
- What does "good work" look like here?
- Any ongoing projects you should be aware of?

### Set Yourself Up

- Get access to all tools, drives, dashboards
- Organise your notes, docs, and logins
- Save important contacts

### Observe the Culture

- Work timings: what's real vs what's said
- Meeting style: formal or casual?
- How decisions are actually made

### What to Do (Not Just Observe)

- Introduce yourself to at least 5 people
- Send a short intro message/email to your team (if it feels right)
- Ask your manager: "What would a great first week look like?"
- Write everything down — you will forget

## First 30 Days — Week-by-Week Playbook

- **WEEK 1: Observe**
    - Build the real power map.
    - Find the person with the map.
    - Say nothing you wouldn't say publicly.
- **WEEK 2: Establish**
    - Make one specific point per meeting.
    - Confirm instructions in writing.
    - Begin one relationship outside your team.
- **WEEK 3: Contribute**
    - Share one insight with your manager.
    - Ask one process question that shows depth.
    - Create one moment of visible reliability.
- **WEEK 4: Position**
    - Identify one problem to solve proactively.
    - Make your thinking visible.
    - Set the tone for month two.

## Before Joining — 7 Points to Read

1. **Offer Letter Clauses:** Ensure the salary structure, probation rules, and working hours are all clearly defined.
2. **CTC vs. In-Hand Breakdown:** Do not just be happy hearing a figure like "5 LPA" (5 Lakhs Per Annum); check exactly how much you will receive in-hand.
3. **Bond Agreement:** Beware of "bond traps" where you are tied down for one or two years in the name of training.
4. **Notice Period:** Check if the notice period is 30, 90, or 180 days so you do not find yourself stuck later.
5. **Job Role and Responsibilities:** Understand what your actual role will be — whether it involves real skill development or just basic Excel work.
6. **Leave Policy:** Details regarding casual leaves and sick leaves should be clearly mentioned.
7. **Exit Policy:** A very common clause allows a company to fire you easily, while making it difficult for you to leave.
