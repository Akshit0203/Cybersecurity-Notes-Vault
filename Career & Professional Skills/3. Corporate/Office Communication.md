## Corporate Communication — Say It Right

| What You Want to Say | How to Say It in Corporate |
|---|---|
| "Can I get a promotion?" | "I'd love to talk about what career development could look like within our team." |
| "Uh, I don't know." | "That's a great question. Let me confirm and get back to you on that." |
| "That's not my job." | "I'm happy to help. Right now, I have a couple of priorities that are on my list. However, I can take a look at it next week." |
| "I just say what I want to say." | **Knowing how to say what you want to say in corporate is really important.** |

## The Golden Rule — If It's Not on Mail, It Never Happened

> Corporate reality check:
> If it's not on mail, it never happened.
> People will forget, twist words, or deny conversations — and you'll be left explaining.
>
> Document everything. It's not fear. It's survival.

## Things That Quietly Ruin Your Image at Work

> No one tells you… but everyone notices.

1. **Missing deadlines again and again** — One delay is fine… a pattern becomes your reputation.
2. **Being distracted in meetings** — Checking mails, typing… while someone's talking. Been there once… got called out instantly.
3. **Taking too long to reply** — Even a "noted" shows you're serious. Silence looks careless.
4. **Complaining about everything** — If you only point out problems… you start sounding like one.
5. **Getting involved in gossip** — Feels normal… until it backfires on you. People assume you talk the same way about them too.
6. **Careless communication** — Typos, rushed messages, unclear instructions… it quietly questions your attention to detail.
7. **Showing up late or unprepared** — Not once… but repeatedly. That's how your image is built.

> **Truth:** Your reputation at work isn't built in big moments… it's built in small daily habits.

## Immature Workplace Habits That Damage Your Professional Image

1. **Calling your manager "Bhai," "bhaiya," "didi," or "bro" in office** — It may feel respectful culturally, but in corporate settings it can blur boundaries and reduce perceived professionalism.
2. **Acting overly comfortable too soon** — Just because the environment feels friendly doesn't mean hierarchy disappears.
3. **Treating feedback like a personal attack** — In reality, feedback is about performance — not about you as a person.
4. **Expecting special treatment because you're "close"** — Professional growth depends on results, not emotional bonding.
5. **Oversharing personal life details during work hours** — Colleagues are coworkers first — not personal support systems.
6. **Using informal language in official emails or meetings** — Tone reflects maturity. Casual words can reduce credibility.
7. **Publicly joking with seniors the same way you joke with friends** — What feels funny to you may look disrespectful to others.
8. **Ignoring formal communication channels** — Skipping process because "you know them personally" creates confusion.
9. **Getting too emotionally attached to workplace relationships** — Corporate equations can change anytime — roles stay, emotions don't.
10. **Forgetting that reputation builds slowly but breaks quickly** — Small habits like how you address people shape long-term career perception.

## 3 Places to Never Share Secrets at Work

> The only talk that remains secret is the one that you didn't say! Keep this in mind.

Most career damage doesn't happen because of incompetence…
It happens because of careless conversations.
Be smart about where you speak.
Comfort zones in office are rarely confidential zones.

There are **three places in your office where you should never ever share anything confidential or secret**:

1. **The Washroom** — An employee once told a colleague in the washroom that they were taking a sick leave the following day to combine it with a Monday public holiday for a four-day break, rather than using privileged leave. Unfortunately, their manager was in one of the vestibules and overheard everything. In the subsequent team meeting, the manager announced that no sick leaves would be permitted the next day due to a payroll cutoff, preventing any unplanned leave.
2. **The Smoking Zone** — This is a place where people relax and vent their frustrations, often leading them to overshare. What feels like personal bonding can quickly become office news within hours.
3. **In Front of the Wrong Audience** — You may share something with a colleague while someone from another team is sitting or standing nearby. While it may seem harmless, you do not know who that person is close to; they might be close to your manager, allowing your secret to travel.

> Always remember: at workplaces, **"even the walls have ears"**.
