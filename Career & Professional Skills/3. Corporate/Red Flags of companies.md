# Red Flags of Companies

---

## The "Perfect Company" Scam

On the surface, everything looks great:

- Money is good
- Everyone wants to go into this company
- Name and fame of the company is very good
- In their award shows, people are getting awards
- Employees are saying "my manager is so good, I can't even believe it"
- Managers are saying good things about employees — "he is so good"
- Teammates are appreciating everyone
- The food is so good
- They give work from home options, leave options, company supports you legally

**Even then…**

### The Reality Behind the Curtain

- The actual work is not good
- Politics is too much in the company
- Even after everything, the company is a scam

### How to Recognize It

- What employees were saying about their manager was **in a sarcastic way** — but it's up to you to recognize that
- Same with peer-to-peer employee praise

### What Happens When You Complain to HR

- Everything will be a show-off of consoling you only
- They will say: "He is new, that's why he doesn't know how the system works"
- "It works like this only"
- "Don't try to change the system"
- When everyone is corrupt, no one will help you

---

## Lesson from My First Year of Working

> Really unfortunate to hear, but I have learnt one thing in my first year when I started working — **no one thinks about your betterment**. It's me who has to fight for my position, work, duties, and anything. If I get attached to the job or company, it will not benefit my personal growth. And that's the same I advise to everyone who starts working new: **you are just a number**. Never attach yourself to the job. Give yourself plenty of time to relax and discover yourself.

---

## You Are Not Family to Your Company

Not because I am special. But because I stopped waiting for a company to reward me. I stopped believing I was part of a family.

In India, we get attached. We call our team our family. We sacrifice our weekends. We skip family dinners. We think *company humare apne hain*. But jab unko cost cut karna hota hai, they do it in one mail. Simple.

**That is when I learned the truth:** You are not family to your company. You are an employee. And that is okay.

- Work hard
- Learn more
- Save more
- Invest more
- Move when you need to
- Do what is right for you

> Companies replace people every day. So work with heart — but keep your head clear.
