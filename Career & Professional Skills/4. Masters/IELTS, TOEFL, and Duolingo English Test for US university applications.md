Why I Chose Duolingo

**1. Cost-effective:** At $65, it's literally 1/3 the price of TOEFL/IELTS. When you're already spending thousands on applications, this matters.

**2. Convenience:** I took it from my bedroom at 11 PM in my pajamas. No commuting to test centers, no waking up early on a Saturday.

**3. Fast results:** Got my scores in 48 hours. My friend waited a week for TOEFL scores and nearly missed a deadline.

**4. Free unlimited score sends:** This is HUGE. I sent my scores to 15+ universities at no extra cost. With TOEFL/IELTS, that would've cost me hundreds of dollars in additional fees.

**5. Learning curve:** The practice tests are free and give you a real feel for the format. I spent about a week preparing using the free resources, and felt completely ready.

**6. Fair grading:** The test is AI-graded with human review. I was worried about bias, but the scoring felt accurate and consistent with my English level.

# Important Considerations!!

- **Acceptance:** This is critical – not all universities accept Duolingo yet, though most major US universities now do (including Ivies, top UCs, etc.). **Always check your target schools' websites first.** Some programs still prefer IELTS/TOEFL, especially at the graduate level.
    
- **Recognition:** IELTS and TOEFL have been around longer and are more universally recognized, especially outside the US. If you're applying to UK, Australia, or Canadian schools alongside US schools, IELTS might be safer.
    
- **Test format:** Duolingo's adaptive format means the test gets harder or easier based on your performance. Some people love this, others find it stressful not knowing how they're doing.
    
- **Speaking component:** Duolingo has you record video responses that are sent to schools. IELTS has a face-to-face speaking test with an examiner. TOEFL has recorded speaking responses but no video. Choose based on your comfort level.
    

# My Honest Take

For US university applications specifically, if your schools accept it, Duolingo is the no-brainer choice. It saved me money, time, and stress. The test itself felt fair and comprehensive – it tested reading, writing, listening, and speaking just like the others.

That said, do your homework. Check each university's requirements. Some programs explicitly state "TOEFL or IELTS only" while others say "any recognized English proficiency test."

**Pro tip:** Some schools that "prefer" TOEFL/IELTS will still accept Duolingo – check their website or email the admissions office if you're unsure.
