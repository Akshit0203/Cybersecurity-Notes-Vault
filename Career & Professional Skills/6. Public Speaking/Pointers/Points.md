# Points — Cybersecurity Career & Mindset

---

## The Simple Loop

- It's a simple loop: If you don't know the work, I've told you where to learn it. Then try to get a good job. Then learn more. Then apply again.
- That's the entire loop.
- Where did this idea come from that "there are no jobs" or "the market is down"?
- The truth is: you don't know the work that companies actually need.
- So first, stop blaming the outside world — put the blame on yourself.

---

## Anything Is Possible — Just Reach Out

- If someone wants to work in government cyber cells — connections will help. And if you're already interning at a cyber cell, all the relevant people are right there. Tell them what work you've done.
- If you want to work at DRDO — email the scientists directly.
- If you want good offers to come to you on their own — build and maintain your LinkedIn profile.
- We just keep overcomplicating things: "How will I get a good job?", "How much will I earn?", "What will happen next?"
- But nobody actually finds joy in the work itself.

### The 3 Stages of Career Interest

1. **Interest in money, name, or fame.**
2. **Interest in a specific role** — like Security Analyst, Security Engineer, Penetration Tester, SOC Analyst, Threat Hunter, Malware Analyst, etc.
3. **Interest in the work itself** — "I do penetration testing because I genuinely love it." If you're in malware analysis, you want to know how malware actually works at a root level, what the biggest malwares ever built were, how they operated. This third option automatically unlocks the other two.

## Concepts Over Tools — How to Apply This Thinking Daily

- You may have heard people say: "Don't focus on tools, focus on concepts." But not everyone truly understands what that means.
- Here's how to bring this kind of thinking into your daily life:

### Mock Interview Approach

- We'll do mock interviews where I'll ask heavy cross-questions.
- I need volunteers for this.
- These are actual interview questions — the kind that will get you a 1 lakh/month job.
- Not just definitions or small commands.

### The Hard Truth About Traditional Tools

- If you're still just using tools — running Kali Linux, running Burp Suite, sitting and doing L1 SOC work, manually checking alerts — you are going to become outdated very soon. This sounds harsh, but it's the truth.
- **The solution:** Integrate AI into whatever you do.
- Whatever work you do, just think: "How would I automate this if I had to?"

### AI Is Not a Big Deal Anymore — Learn It Step by Step

- Don't make AI into something scary like "This is not my field, how will I learn this?"
- Break it down step by step: First Python → then Deep Learning → then ML → then Generative AI → then Agentic AI → then what happens when AGI arrives and when.

### Stop Underestimating Yourself

- Another mistake we make: when we apply somewhere, we think too little of ourselves — "I don't know this work," "I have no experience," and we give ourselves false comfort: "Look at the competition, thousands of people apply, why would I get selected?"
- But the opposite is very logical: if you don't apply at all, how will it ever happen?
- There's no harm in taking a shot. I've taken many shots, and many have worked out.

## Stop Blaming the Market — Look Inward

- We keep blaming the outside world: "The cybersecurity market is bad," "Salaries aren't good," "I'm not getting selected." But that was never the real question.
- The real question was: **Do I actually know the work, or am I just going through the motions?**
- If you want to work in companies or government — do you actually know what they need?
- This applies to everyone in their relevant fields.

### The Reality Check Call

- I get many LinkedIn messages from people saying: "Akshit, I know everything, but I'm still not getting a job."
- Then when I spend half an hour on a call with them, asking cross-questions about what they actually know — it turns out they only know theory, or their practical knowledge is outdated.
- Our field changes every single day.

### Cybersecurity Doesn't Need Hard DSA

- In cybersecurity, you don't literally need high-level DSA where people were solving dynamic programming problems 10 years ago and are still doing the same ones today.
- In our field, something new comes every day. A new domain emerges, and that new domain's security needs to be addressed.

### Example: Network Security in the AI Era

- If someone says "I do network security" — what will you do first? Reconnaissance.
- But what if there's no SQL database running, no SSH port open, no FTP, no RDP — and if they are open, all vulnerabilities are patched? What will you do then?
- What if only an AI system is running there, and it's completely vulnerable? Because most people haven't learned agentic AI security yet — most can't do it 100% reliably.
- The entire landscape has changed. These new AI systems have different ports, different services entirely.
- So when you scan and see a port or vulnerability you've never seen before — you can't just say "I don't care until a web app is running." You can't just abandon it.

### Expand Your Scope

- Infrastructure is slowly moving to the cloud — so cloud security is important.
- Don't limit yourself: "I'll only learn this one domain in cybersecurity."
- For example, the web app you're testing — where is it deployed now? DevOps concepts come into play. You might find a Docker file for the entire web application with a bug in it.
- Keep gradually expanding what you learn — doors will keep opening. Wherever you go to work, you'll have an edge over everyone else.

## The Future Is AI — Traditional Approaches Are Getting Outdated

We generally think about which is the best field in cybersecurity — Blue Team, Red Team, SOC, Purple Team, Forensics, etc.

But the truth is that gradually, all of these are becoming outdated.

Right now you might think: "But I'm doing penetration testing!" or "I want to get a SOC job!" — but the reality is that as AI grows, traditional approaches are becoming outdated.

You might not understand this right now. But someday in the future, when you're stressed — when you can't find a job, can't get an internship, or your growth has stalled — you'll remember this and understand why it's happening.

**So always try to stay at the forefront of cybersecurity — stay updated on what's actually happening in the industry.**

The best direction is clearly moving towards AI. AI will transform almost everything.

If you keep doing only basic things — just using tools or doing the same traditional work — you'll be left behind.

**Come out of your comfort zone:**

- Learn AI
- Learn Cloud
- Learn Cloud Security

Whatever field you like — whether it's Forensics, Malware, SOC, or Penetration Testing — **integrate AI into it.**

The faster you adapt, the better you'll survive in the future. Otherwise, the "general path" everyone is following will slowly become saturated and outdated… and people will just be left sitting with nothing.

## Which Jobs Are at Risk?

The speaker argues that any job that does not require active "thinking" or involves performing the same repetitive tasks daily is at risk of being automated.

- **The "Repetition" Warning:** A former manager at Sandvine once advised that if you go to the office every day knowing exactly what needs to be done and how to do it, you are in a precarious position.
- **Automation of Simple Tasks:** Repetitive tasks in service-based roles — such as clicking the same buttons or performing routine front-end adjustments like changing a button's color or shape — are already effectively automated or "gone."

## The Value of Critical Thinking and Creativity

The speaker emphasizes that AI's primary limitation is its inability to "think" independently; rather, it functions as a massive database.

- **AI vs. Human Creativity:** AI can paint a horse very well because it has a database of existing horse paintings. However, if no one had ever painted a dragon, the AI would not know how to start, whereas a human can "think" and imagine how to create one.
- **Safe Jobs:** Your job is considered safe if you go to work without a clear "clue" as to exactly what challenges you will face or how you will solve them, as this requires the problem-solving skills that AI lacks.

## The Reality of the Job Market

Despite the marketing claims from AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude that they are "ending" front-end or back-end development, the speaker points out a significant irony: these very companies continue to list numerous openings for front-end and back-end engineers on their own career sites. The core message is that **jobs are not disappearing**, but the demand is shifting toward those who can **increase their capacity to think** rather than just execute routine code.
