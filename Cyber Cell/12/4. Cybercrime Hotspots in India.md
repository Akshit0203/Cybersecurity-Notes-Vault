
# Cybercrime Hotspots in India

# 1. Overview

Certain regions in India have gained attention due to repeated involvement in specific types of cyber-enabled crimes. These are often referred to as **cybercrime hotspots**, although cybercrime can originate from anywhere.

Many hotspots specialize in particular fraud techniques based on local criminal networks.

---

# 2. Jamtara, Jharkhand

Jamtara became one of India's most well-known cybercrime hotspots.

Common crimes:

- Phishing scams
    
- OTP fraud
    
- Bank KYC scams
    
- Debit/Credit card fraud
    
- UPI fraud
    
- Fake customer care scams
    
- SIM swap fraud
    

Typical modus operandi:

```text
Victim

↓

Fake Bank Call

↓

OTP / Card Details

↓

Unauthorized Banking Transaction
```

---

# 3. Howrah (West Bengal)

Howrah has been linked in various investigations to:

- Digital arrest scams
    
- Investment scams
    
- Fake customer support scams
    
- WhatsApp scams
    
- Online marketplace fraud
    
- Banking fraud
    

Cybercrime cells have conducted multiple operations targeting organized fraud networks in the region.

---

# 4. Mewat Region (Haryana–Rajasthan Border)

The Mewat region (including parts of **Nuh district in Haryana** and nearby areas) has been identified in investigations involving:

- Sextortion
    
- Video call blackmail
    
- Honey-trap scams
    
- Fake social media profiles
    
- Digital arrest impersonation
    
- Fake police/CBI calls
    

Typical workflow:

```text
Fake Social Media Profile

↓

Video Call

↓

Screen Recording

↓

Blackmail

↓

Online Payment
```

---

# 5. Bharatpur, Rajasthan

Bharatpur has become a major hotspot for:

- Sextortion
    
- Fake police impersonation
    
- OLX/online marketplace scams
    
- Digital arrest scams
    
- QR code scams
    
- Fake investment scams
    
- SIM-related fraud
    

Law enforcement agencies have carried out numerous operations against organized cyber fraud gangs operating from the district.

# 7. Investigation Techniques

Law enforcement agencies commonly use:

- Call Detail Records (CDRs)
    
- IPDR analysis
    
- Tower dumps
    
- UPI transaction tracing
    
- Bank account analysis
    
- Mule account identification
    
- Cryptocurrency analysis
    
- Device forensics
    
- Mobile forensics
    
- OSINT
    
- Financial intelligence
    

---

# 8. Why These Areas Become Hotspots

Contributing factors may include:

- Organized criminal networks
    
- Availability of money mule accounts
    
- Easy access to digital payment platforms
    
- Social engineering techniques
    
- Cross-state operations
    
- Rapid movement of fraudulent funds
    
- Recruitment of local operatives
    

---

# 9. Prevention

Citizens should:

- Never share OTPs or banking credentials.
    
- Verify callers claiming to be from banks or government agencies.
    
- Be cautious of investment schemes promising guaranteed returns.
    
- Ignore threats of "digital arrest"—legitimate agencies do not demand payments over phone or video calls.
    
- Report cyber fraud immediately by calling **1930** or using the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.
    

---

# 10. Key Takeaways

- **Jamtara (Jharkhand)** is widely associated with **phishing, OTP, and banking fraud**.
    
- **Howrah (West Bengal)** has been linked to **investment fraud, digital arrest scams, and organized online financial fraud**.
    
- The **Mewat region (Haryana–Rajasthan border)** is known for **sextortion, honey-trap scams, and impersonation fraud**.
    
- **Bharatpur (Rajasthan)** has been identified as a hotspot for **sextortion, fake police scams, QR code fraud, and online marketplace fraud**.
    
- These hotspots are the focus of coordinated investigations by state cybercrime units, central agencies, and financial institutions, but cybercrime is **not limited to these locations** and can originate from anywhere.
