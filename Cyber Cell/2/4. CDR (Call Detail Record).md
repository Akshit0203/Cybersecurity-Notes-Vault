
# CDR (Call Detail Record)

## What Is a CDR?

CDR stands for:

**Call Detail Record**

A CDR is a telecom-generated record that contains metadata about calls and SMS activity.

Important:

A CDR does **not** contain the actual conversation.

It contains information about:

- Who communicated
    
- When communication occurred
    
- Duration
    
- Cell towers used
    
- Device identifiers
    

---

# Typical Fields Found in a CDR

## Mobile Number

The subscriber number under investigation.

Example:

+91XXXXXXXXXX

---

## IMEI

**International Mobile Equipment Identity**

Identifies the physical device.

Example:

356XXXXXXXXXXXXX

Uses:

- Device tracking
    
- Stolen phone investigations
    
- SIM swapping analysis
    

---

## IMSI

**International Mobile Subscriber Identity**

Identifies the SIM card/subscriber.

Stored inside the SIM card.

Uses:

- Subscriber identification
    
- SIM change analysis
    
- Network authentication
    

---

## B-Party Number

The other party involved in communication.

Example:

A-Party (Suspect)

↓

B-Party (Contact)

---

## Duration

Length of the call.

Example:

- 5 seconds
    
- 2 minutes
    
- 45 minutes
    

Useful for:

- Pattern analysis
    
- Relationship mapping
    

---

## Call Type

Indicates the nature of communication.

### O/C

Outgoing Call

Suspect called another number.

---

### I/C

Incoming Call

Suspect received a call.

---

### O/S

Outgoing SMS

SMS sent from suspect's number.

---

### I/S

Incoming SMS

SMS received by suspect's number.

---

## Date and Time

Shows:

- Exact timestamp
    
- Start time
    
- End time
    

Used for:

- Timeline reconstruction
    
- Crime correlation
    

---

# Cell ID Information

One of the most valuable fields.

CDRs often contain:

### Cell ID 1

Serving tower at call initiation.

### Cell ID 2

Tower used during movement or handover.

### Cell ID 3

Subsequent tower if mobility occurred.

---

## Why Cell IDs Matter

Cell IDs help determine:

- Approximate location
    
- Movement pattern
    
- Travel route
    

Example:

8:00 PM

Tower A

↓

8:10 PM

Tower B

↓

8:20 PM

Tower C

This suggests movement between locations.

---

# What Investigators Can Learn

## Communication Analysis

Identify:

- Frequent contacts
    
- Unknown associates
    
- Communication frequency
    

---

## Link Analysis

Determine:

- Criminal networks
    
- Associates
    
- Common contacts
    

---

## Location Analysis

Determine:

- Presence at crime scene
    
- Travel patterns
    
- Meeting locations
    

---

## Timeline Reconstruction

Example:

8:00 PM → Call made

8:03 PM → Call ended

8:05 PM → Tower changed

8:10 PM → SMS sent

This creates a detailed activity timeline.

# CDR Retention

Telecom operators generally maintain CDRs according to regulatory and operational requirements.

In investigations, agencies may seek historical CDR data subject to:

- Applicable laws
    
- Regulatory requirements
    
- Operator retention policies
    

The exact retention period varies by jurisdiction, operator, and legal framework.

---

# Limitations of CDR

CDR typically does NOT provide:

❌ Call recordings

❌ WhatsApp message contents

❌ Telegram chats

❌ Signal messages

❌ Internet browsing history

❌ Email contents

For internet-based applications, investigators often require:

- IPDR (Internet Protocol Detail Records)
    
- Device forensics
    
- Cloud records
    

---

# Key Takeaway

CDRs are one of the most powerful investigative tools because they reveal communication patterns, device identifiers, timestamps, and cell tower locations. Even without call content, investigators can reconstruct relationships, movement patterns, timelines, and criminal networks using CDR analysis and cell tower correlation.
