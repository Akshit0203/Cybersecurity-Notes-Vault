# BNSS Section 94, Privacy Rights, Electronic Records, and Website Defacement Investigation Notes

## BNSS Section 94 – Production of Electronic Records

BNSS stands for:

**Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023**

Section 94 may be used during lawful investigations to obtain electronic records from service providers and intermediaries.

Examples of records that may be requested:

- Subscriber information
    
- Server logs
    
- Platform records
    
- Traffic data
    
- Account information
    
- Digital evidence
    

Examples of service providers:

- Instagram
    
- Facebook
    
- Google
    
- Telegram
    
- Internet Service Providers (ISPs)
    
- Hosting providers
    

Investigators may seek:

- IP logs
    
- Login history
    
- Registration details
    
- Traffic records
    
- Account metadata
    

through procedures authorized by law.

## Right to Privacy and Access to Mobile Phones

In India, the right to privacy is recognized as a fundamental right under Article 21 of the Constitution.

Whether police can access a mobile phone depends on the facts of the case and applicable legal procedures.

Important points:

- Police investigations must follow legal procedures.
    
- Digital devices may be examined during lawful investigations.
    
- Courts may review the legality of searches and seizures.
    
- Individuals have constitutional and legal protections regarding privacy.
    

The exact requirement for a court order depends on the circumstances, the applicable law, and judicial directions in a particular case.

## Complaints, Evidence, and Investigation

A criminal investigation does not always depend solely on a private complaint.

Investigations may begin based on:

- A complaint
    
- Information received by police
    
- Digital evidence
    
- Reports from intermediaries
    
- Cybercrime reports
    
- Other legally recognized sources
    

Whether an offence is established depends on the available evidence and applicable law.

---

## Punishment and Imprisonment

The question of whether a person may face:

- Up to 7 years imprisonment
    
- Up to 10 years imprisonment
    
- Up to 11 years imprisonment
    
- Or another punishment
    

depends entirely on:

- The specific offence charged
    
- The applicable statute
    
- The facts of the case
    
- The evidence available
    
- Court findings
    

There is no single rule that automatically results in a 7-year or 11-year sentence merely because data was obtained or examined.

Only a court can determine guilt and impose punishment according to law.

## Intermediary Guidelines (India)

An intermediary is a platform that hosts, stores, or transmits user content.

Examples:

- Social media platforms
    
- Messaging services
    
- Hosting providers
    
- Cloud providers
    

Examples include:

- Instagram
    
- Facebook
    
- X (Twitter)
    
- YouTube
    
- Google
    

Intermediaries may be required to:

- Preserve records
    
- Respond to lawful requests
    
- Cooperate with investigations
    
- Remove unlawful content when legally required

## AI Images and Fake Identity Documents

Modern AI tools can generate:

- Fake IDs
    
- Fake passports
    
- Fake driving licences
    
- Synthetic faces
    
- Deepfakes
    

Investigations typically focus on:

- Source accounts
    
- Upload IP addresses
    
- Device information
    
- Platform logs
    
- Email registration details
    

rather than the image alone.
