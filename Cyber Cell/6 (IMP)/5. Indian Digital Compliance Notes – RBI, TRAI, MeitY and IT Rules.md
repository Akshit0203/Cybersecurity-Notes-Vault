# Indian Digital Compliance Notes – RBI, TRAI, MeitY and IT Rules

# 1. RBI (Reserve Bank of India)

The RBI issues regulations and directions relating to:

- Digital payments
    
- UPI ecosystem
    
- Banking cybersecurity
    
- Fraud prevention
    
- Customer protection
    
- KYC and AML compliance
    

Banks and regulated entities are expected to implement robust security controls, incident reporting, and fraud monitoring.

---

# 2. TRAI (Telecom Regulatory Authority of India)

TRAI regulates India's telecommunications sector and has initiatives related to:

- SMS header verification
    
- DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology) for commercial messaging
    
- Spam reduction
    
- Telecom consumer protection
    
- Caller identification and messaging transparency
    

The TRAI SMS Header portal allows users to verify registered SMS headers and certain 1600-series communications.

---

# 3. MeitY (Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology)

MeitY is responsible for major digital governance initiatives, including:

- Information Technology Rules
    
- Intermediary Guidelines
    
- Digital platform regulation
    
- AI-related policy initiatives
    
- Online safety measures
    
- Grievance redressal frameworks
    

---

# 4. IT Rules Amendments and Faster Takedown Timelines

Recent amendments and guidance from MeitY have introduced shorter compliance timelines for certain categories of harmful content.

Notable examples include:

- **2-hour timeline** for intermediaries to remove or disable access to specified categories of complained-of intimate or impersonation-related content under Rule 3(2)(b), replacing the earlier longer timeline. This is intended to provide rapid victim protection.
    
- **3-hour timeline** for compliance with certain lawful government notices relating to specified unlawful information under the amended framework.
    

These obligations arise from the IT Rules and associated amendments and FAQs published by MeitY, not from TRAI or RBI.

---

# 5. G.S.R. Notifications

**G.S.R.** stands for **General Statutory Rules**.

A G.S.R. notification is an official notification published in the Gazette of India to bring rules or amendments into force under the authority of an Act of Parliament.

Example:

```text
Parliament Passes Law
        │
        ▼
Ministry Frames Rules
        │
        ▼
G.S.R. Notification Published
        │
        ▼
Rules Become Effective
```

---

# 6. "2-Hour Removal Rule"

Under the updated MeitY framework:

If a complaint relates to specified categories such as certain non-consensual intimate imagery or similar protected categories covered by Rule 3(2)(b), intermediaries are expected to remove or disable access **within 2 hours** of receiving the complaint. This represents a reduction from the previous 24-hour requirement.

---

# 7. Does Facebook or Another Platform Get Automatically Arrested if It Misses the Deadline?

**No.**

There is **no rule stating that Facebook or another intermediary is automatically arrested** if content is not removed within the prescribed timeline.

Possible consequences for non-compliance may include:

- Regulatory scrutiny
    
- Loss of statutory protections in certain circumstances
    
- Court proceedings
    
- Directions from competent authorities
    
- Penalties or other legal consequences as provided by law
    

Criminal liability, if any, depends on the applicable legal provisions and facts of the case; it is **not an automatic arrest mechanism**.

# Key Takeaways

- **RBI** regulates banking and payment systems.
    
- **TRAI** regulates telecom services and messaging frameworks.
    
- **MeitY** administers India's IT Rules and intermediary obligations.
    
- **G.S.R.** means **General Statutory Rules**, published in the Gazette to notify rules or amendments.
    
- Recent MeitY amendments shortened certain takedown timelines, including a **2-hour requirement for specified complaint categories** and **3-hour compliance for certain government directions**.
    
- Missing these timelines does **not** automatically mean that executives of platforms like Facebook will be arrested; enforcement depends on the applicable legal framework and due process.
