# SEBI CSCRF (Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework)

# 1. What is CSCRF?

**CSCRF** stands for **Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework**.

It was introduced by the **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** on **20 August 2024** to establish a unified cybersecurity framework for SEBI-regulated entities.

---

# 2. Objective

The framework aims to:

- Strengthen cybersecurity governance.
    
- Improve cyber resilience.
    
- Standardize cybersecurity requirements across the securities market.
    
- Protect investors and financial infrastructure.
    
- Improve incident response and recovery.
    

---

# 3. Why is CSCRF Important?

CSCRF is considered **one of the most comprehensive cybersecurity regulatory frameworks issued by an Indian financial regulator** because it consolidates earlier sector-specific cyber requirements into a single standards-based framework and aligns with international best practices.

---

# 4. Who Must Comply?

The framework applies to **SEBI Regulated Entities (REs)**, including:

- Stock Exchanges
    
- Depositories
    
- Clearing Corporations
    
- Stock Brokers
    
- Depository Participants
    
- Mutual Funds
    
- Asset Management Companies (AMCs)
    
- Portfolio Managers
    
- Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs)
    
- Investment Advisers
    
- Research Analysts
    
- KYC Registration Agencies
    
- Other SEBI-regulated entities
    

The applicable requirements depend on the entity's category and risk profile.

---

# 5. Core Objectives of CSCRF

The framework is built around five resilience objectives:

```text
Anticipate

↓

Withstand

↓

Contain

↓

Recover

↓

Evolve
```

These objectives are inspired by CERT-In's Cyber Crisis Management approach.

---

# 6. Major Technical Requirements

The framework covers:

- Cybersecurity governance
    
- Board oversight
    
- Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
    
- Asset inventory
    
- Risk assessment
    
- Network security
    
- Endpoint security
    
- Identity and Access Management (IAM)
    
- API security
    
- Vulnerability Assessment & Penetration Testing (VAPT)
    
- Security Operations Centre (SOC)
    
- Threat intelligence
    
- Security monitoring
    
- Incident response
    
- Disaster Recovery (DR)
    
- Business Continuity Planning (BCP)
    
- Audit and compliance
    
- Cloud security
    
- Supply chain security
    
- Software Bill of Materials (SBOM)
    

---

# 7. Security Operations Centre (SOC)

CSCRF requires continuous security monitoring through:

- Organization's own SOC
    
- Group SOC
    
- Managed SOC
    
- Market-SOC (M-SOC) for eligible smaller entities
    

SOC functions include:

- 24×7 monitoring
    
- SIEM
    
- Threat detection
    
- Incident management
    
- Log analysis
    
- Threat intelligence
    

---

# 8. Incident Response

Entities must establish documented procedures for:

```text
Detection

↓

Containment

↓

Eradication

↓

Recovery

↓

Post-Incident Review
```

Timely reporting and response are mandatory under the framework.

---

# 9. Risk-Based Compliance

CSCRF classifies regulated entities based on factors such as:

- Size
    
- Assets under management
    
- Trading activity
    
- Operational importance
    
- Systemic risk
    

Larger or more critical entities have more stringent obligations.

---

# 10. International Standards Alignment

CSCRF references or aligns with widely recognized practices such as:

- ISO/IEC 27001
    
- NIST guidance
    
- CIS Controls
    
- CERT-In guidance
    

This helps create a consistent cybersecurity baseline across India's securities ecosystem.

---

# 11. Benefits

- Standardized cybersecurity controls
    
- Stronger governance
    
- Better cyber resilience
    
- Improved incident response
    
- Continuous monitoring
    
- Reduced cyber risk
    
- Better protection for investors and market infrastructure
    

---

# 12. Key Takeaways

- **CSCRF (Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework)** was issued by **SEBI** on **20 August 2024**.
    
- It establishes a comprehensive cybersecurity framework for **SEBI-regulated entities**.
    
- The framework emphasizes governance, continuous monitoring, SOC operations, incident response, resilience, and alignment with recognized cybersecurity standards.
    
- It is widely regarded as **one of the most comprehensive cybersecurity frameworks issued by an Indian financial regulator**, although "most rigorous in India" is an opinion rather than an official designation.
