
# Karnataka High Court: Google Reviews Are Not Proof of Criminal History

## Background

In a criminal case, the prosecution argued that the accused was a habitual offender because Google searches and Google reviews contained allegations that he had cheated multiple people.

The prosecution attempted to use these online reviews and internet search results to oppose relief sought by the accused.

---

## Core Legal Question

Can Google reviews, online comments, or Google search results be treated as legal evidence proving that a person has a criminal history?

---

## Court's Finding

The Karnataka High Court held that:

> Google reviews and Google search results do not have legal evidentiary value for proving criminal antecedents or criminal history.

The Court observed that such material cannot be treated as proof that a person committed offences in the past.

---

## Why Did the Court Reject Google Reviews?

Anyone can post:

- Anonymous reviews
    
- Fake reviews
    
- Malicious reviews
    
- Competitor reviews
    
- Unverified allegations
    

The identity and truthfulness of the author may not be established.

Therefore:

Google Reviews

≠ Proof of Crime

Google Search Results

≠ Criminal Record

Internet Allegations

≠ Admissible Evidence

---

## What Can Be Used to Prove Criminal History?

Courts generally rely on:

### Official Criminal Records

- FIRs
    
- Chargesheets
    
- Court records
    
- Conviction records
    

### Witness Testimony

- Statements
    
- Depositions
    
- Cross-examination
    

### Electronic Evidence

- Properly collected digital evidence
    
- Logs
    
- Documents
    
- Records proved according to law
    

---

## Investigation Perspective

Online information may still be useful.

Investigators can use:

- Google reviews
    
- Social media posts
    
- Public complaints
    
- Internet discussions
    

as intelligence leads.

However:

A lead is not evidence.

The information must be independently verified through lawful investigation.

---

## Example

Google Review Says:

"This person cheated me."

Investigator cannot conclude:

"Accused is a fraudster."

Instead investigators must ask:

- Was a complaint filed?
    
- Is there an FIR?
    
- Is there evidence?
    
- Are there witnesses?
    
- Can the allegation be verified?
    

Only then can the allegation become part of a criminal case.

---

## Important Legal Principle

### Intelligence vs Evidence

Intelligence:

- Tips
    
- Online reviews
    
- Rumours
    
- Social media posts
    

Used to start investigation.

Evidence:

- Verified facts
    
- Documents
    
- Witnesses
    
- Official records
    

Used to prove facts in court.

Courts decide cases based on evidence, not internet reputation.

---

## Why This Case Is Important

The judgment reinforces that:

- Online content is not automatically evidence.
    
- Search engine results do not determine guilt.
    
- Public opinion does not equal criminal history.
    
- Courts require legally admissible proof before treating a person as a habitual offender.
    

---

## Key Takeaway

Google reviews, online complaints, and search engine results may provide investigative leads, but they cannot by themselves establish that a person has a criminal record or criminal history. Criminal antecedents must be proved through legally admissible evidence such as official records, witness testimony, convictions, and properly authenticated documents.
