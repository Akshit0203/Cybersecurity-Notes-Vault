# Case Study: How Investigators Tracked the Source of a Political Smear Campaign Using OSINT, Digital Forensics, CDR, and IPDR

> **Note:** This case study is based on the scenario described and is presented as an educational investigation workflow.

---

# 1. Initial Incident

A senior political leader (minister) contacted investigators after a letter containing **15–20 corruption allegations** against him started spreading online.

### Situation

- Allegations were going viral on social media.
- Pressure was building for the minister to resign.
- Minister claimed the allegations were fabricated.
- Investigators had only **24–48 hours** to identify the source.

### Investigation Objective

Determine:

1. Who created the allegations?
2. Who coordinated the campaign?
3. Who leaked and distributed the document?

---

# 2. Phase 1 – Social Media Intelligence (SOCMINT)

The first step was to analyze public social media activity.

### Tool Used

Sentiment Analysis / Social Media Monitoring

### Actions

Investigators:

- Monitored Facebook activity.
- Collected posts mentioning the minister.
- Identified users:
    - Posting allegations
    - Sharing memes
    - Commenting
    - Liking and promoting content

### Discovery

The campaign had been running for approximately **one month**.

A large number of users promoting the content were actually members of the minister's own political party.

### Key Insight

Conspiracies rarely start randomly.

Usually:

1. A small trusted group initiates the operation.
2. Early supporters amplify it.
3. Larger audiences join later.

Therefore, investigators needed to find the **origin cluster**.

---

# 3. Phase 2 – Profile Collection and Data Aggregation

### Method

Investigators manually collected profiles involved in promoting the content.

### Data Collected

For each profile:

- Name
- Location
- Followers
- Friends
- Party affiliation
- Influence level

### Result

A database of approximately:

**527 Facebook profiles**

was created for analysis.

---

# 4. Phase 3 – Geographic Clustering

### Question

Where did the campaign originate?

### Method

Location analysis of all 527 profiles.

### Finding

Out of 527 profiles:

**145 profiles belonged to the same geographic area**

(called "Dharmapur" in the example).

### Significance

When many influential participants originate from one location, that location becomes an investigation hotspot.

### Correlation

Investigators asked:

> "Did the minister recently visit this area?"

Answer:

**Yes — one month earlier.**

Interestingly:

- The smear campaign also started roughly one month earlier.

This created a timeline correlation.

---

# 5. Phase 4 – Influencer Network Analysis

Investigators then focused only on the 145 profiles.

### Goal

Identify key influencers.

### Criteria

Profiles with:

- Large follower counts
- High engagement
- Strong party connections
- Leadership positions

### Result

17 influential accounts identified.

---

# 6. Phase 5 – Leadership Filtering

Among those 17 accounts, investigators searched for people who had held positions such as:

- Minister
- President
- Vice President
- Secretary
- Party office bearer

### Result

Three high-profile individuals emerged.

### Problem

These were powerful people.

Investigators could not accuse them solely based on social media interactions.

Further evidence was needed.

---

# 7. Phase 6 – Arrest and Unexpected Problem

Authorities arrested one of the suspects.

### What Happened?

Upon seizure:

- Mobile phone was formatted.
- Laptop was formatted.

### Initial Concern

No evidence remained.

### Important Observation

The formatting itself became evidence.

Investigators reasoned:

If both devices were wiped immediately before arrest:

1. Someone likely warned the suspect.
2. The suspect knew the arrest was coming.
3. The suspect had something worth destroying.

This shifted the investigation toward finding:

> Who leaked the arrest information?

---

# 8. Phase 7 – Deleted Data Recovery

### Digital Forensics Performed

Investigators recovered deleted data from:

- Laptop
- Mobile device

### Outcome

The controversial corruption document was recovered.

### Problem

Possession ≠ Creation

The suspect could argue:

> "Someone sent me the file."

Therefore, investigators still needed proof of:

- Authorship
- Distribution
- Coordination

---

# 9. Phase 8 – CDR Analysis

### What is CDR?

Call Detail Records.

CDR typically includes:

- Phone numbers
- Call times
- Duration
- Cell tower information
- Location information

### Limitation

CDRs generally do not reveal WhatsApp call contents.

---

# 10. Critical Discovery from CDR

Investigators found:

### WhatsApp OTP Event

At approximately **11:00 AM**, a WhatsApp OTP was generated.

### Arrest Time

The suspect was arrested around **3:00 PM**.

### Why is This Important?

Receiving a WhatsApp OTP usually means:

- WhatsApp was reinstalled.
- Device was reset.
- Account was reconfigured.

### Logical Deduction

If WhatsApp was reinstalled at 11 AM:

- The phone had already been formatted.
- The suspect knew about the arrest before 11 AM.

---

# 11. Building the Timeline

Investigators established:

|Time|Event|
|---|---|
|Before 11:00 AM|Warning received|
|Before 11:00 AM|Phone formatted|
|11:00 AM|WhatsApp OTP generated|
|3:00 PM|Arrest|

This narrowed the investigation window.

### Window of Interest

**7:00 AM – 11:00 AM**

The warning most likely occurred during this period.

---

# 12. Phase 9 – IPDR Analysis

### What is IPDR?

Internet Protocol Detail Record.

IPDR can show:

- Internet sessions
- App connections
- Service access logs
- Metadata about communications

### Why Use IPDR?

Modern criminals often use:

- WhatsApp
- Signal
- FaceTime
- Telegram

instead of normal phone calls.

CDR alone becomes insufficient.

---

# 13. Narrowing the Search

Instead of reviewing an entire day of internet activity, investigators analyzed only:

### 7 AM – 11 AM

This dramatically reduced the amount of data.

---

# 14. Discovery of WhatsApp Calls

Within the selected window:

Investigators found:

### Two WhatsApp Audio Calls

Between approximately:

**9:00 AM – 9:05 AM**

### Significance

The calls occurred before:

- Device formatting
- OTP generation

Therefore they became highly suspicious.

---

# 15. Identifying Close Contacts

Investigators then mapped the suspect's closest contacts.

### Method

People with frequent communication patterns:

- Family
- Driver
- Secretary
- Staff
- Political workers

### Result

12 close contacts identified.

---

# 16. Cross-Matching IPDR Records

Investigators obtained IPDR records for all 12 contacts.

### Goal

Find who communicated during the critical 9:00–9:05 window.

### Result

One match appeared.

The suspect's driver had a corresponding WhatsApp call.

---

# 17. Extending the Communication Chain

The driver's records showed:

Before calling the suspect,

the driver had received a WhatsApp call from:

### A Police Officer

This was the breakthrough.

The chain now looked like:

Police Officer  
↓  
Driver  
↓  
Suspect

This explained how the suspect learned about the upcoming arrest.

---

# 18. Phase 10 – Driver Device Examination

Investigators seized and analyzed the driver's phone.

### Major Mistake by Conspirators

The main suspect wiped his devices.

The driver did not.

---

# 19. Evidence Recovered

Messages showed that for approximately one month:

The suspect had been directing the driver to:

- Spread allegations
- Share content in groups
- Add additional accusations
- Recruit more participants
- Leak internal information

These messages directly linked the suspect to the campaign.

---

# 20. Final Evidence Chain

Investigators established:

### Social Media Evidence

- Coordinated campaign identified

↓

### Geographic Analysis

- Origin cluster identified

↓

### Network Analysis

- Key influencers isolated

↓

### Device Formatting

- Indication of consciousness of guilt

↓

### Deleted Data Recovery

- Smear document recovered

↓

### CDR Analysis

- WhatsApp reinstallation timeline established

↓

### IPDR Analysis

- Critical WhatsApp calls identified

↓

### Contact Network Analysis

- Driver identified

↓

### Driver Device Examination

- Instructions and coordination messages recovered

↓

### Complete Conspiracy Proven

---

# Key Investigation Techniques Used

1. **SOCMINT (Social Media Intelligence)**
    - Sentiment analysis
    - Profile mapping
2. **Network Analysis**
    - Influence mapping
    - Relationship discovery
3. **Geospatial Analysis**
    - Location clustering
4. **Digital Forensics**
    - Deleted file recovery
    - Device examination
5. **CDR Analysis**
    - Timeline reconstruction
6. **IPDR Analysis**
    - Internet communication correlation
7. **Link Analysis**
    - Communication chain reconstruction
8. **Behavioral Analysis**
    - Interpreting device formatting and suspicious actions

# Major Lesson

The case was not solved by a single piece of evidence. It was solved by combining:

**OSINT + Social Network Analysis + Digital Forensics + CDR + IPDR + Human Intelligence**

Each clue alone was weak, but together they formed a defensible evidence chain that revealed the organizers, the leak source, and the distribution network.
