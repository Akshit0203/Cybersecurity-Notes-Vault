
The National Security Agency (NSA) has two primary missions:

1. **Foreign signals intelligence (SIGINT)** — collecting intelligence from communications and electronic signals.
2. **Information assurance** — protecting U.S. government communications and cybersecurity.

Who they monitor depends on the legal authority, the intelligence objective, and whether the target is inside or outside the United States.

## Primary targets

### 1. Foreign governments (highest priority)

This is the NSA's main mission.

Examples include:

- Military communications
- Diplomatic communications
- Intelligence agencies
- Political leaders
- Defense contractors

The goal is to understand another country's capabilities and intentions.

---

### 2. Terrorist organizations

Examples include groups designated as terrorist organizations by the U.S. government.

They monitor:

- Leadership communications
- Recruitment
- Financing
- Operational planning
- Online propaganda

---

### 3. Foreign military networks

The NSA tries to understand:

- troop movements
- missile programs
- cyber capabilities
- radar systems
- satellite communications

---

### 4. Cyber threat actors

Major targets include:

- State-sponsored hackers
- Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) groups
- Ransomware operators that threaten U.S. interests
- Critical infrastructure attackers

The NSA works closely with agencies like Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency and Federal Bureau of Investigation in some cases.

---

### 5. Foreign intelligence agencies

Examples include agencies from countries such as:

- Russia
- China
- Iran
- North Korea

These agencies are major intelligence priorities because they conduct espionage and cyber operations.

---

### 6. Nuclear and missile programs

They monitor communications related to:

- nuclear weapons
- missile launches
- military testing
- weapons development

---

### 7. International organized crime

Examples:

- drug cartels
- human trafficking networks
- weapons trafficking
- money laundering

---

### 8. Cyber espionage against U.S. companies

If a foreign actor is stealing:

- intellectual property
- aerospace technology
- AI research
- semiconductor designs

those activities can become intelligence targets.

---

## Do they monitor ordinary people?

Generally, **ordinary individuals are not the intended targets** of NSA foreign intelligence collection.

However, there are important nuances:

- If an ordinary person communicates directly with a foreign intelligence target, their communications could be incidentally collected.
- If someone becomes part of a counterterrorism, counterintelligence, or other national security investigation, collection may occur under applicable legal authorities.
- Communications collected under foreign intelligence programs may include incidental information about non-targets, subject to rules governing handling and retention.

For U.S. persons, there are legal restrictions and oversight mechanisms intended to limit how intelligence agencies collect, use, and retain information.

---

## How do they choose targets?

Target selection is driven by intelligence requirements established through the U.S. national security process. Analysts don't simply pick random people. Typical criteria include:

- National security importance
- Foreign intelligence value
- Terrorism links
- Military relevance
- Cyber threat activity
- Counterintelligence concerns
- Proliferation of weapons

Each approved target is generally associated with a specific intelligence requirement.

---

If you're:

- studying cybersecurity,
- running home labs,
- participating in CTFs,
- using Kali Linux,
- researching malware for defensive purposes,

that **does not by itself make you an NSA target**.

The agency's resources are finite and are focused on foreign intelligence priorities and significant national security threats, not typical cybersecurity learners or professionals.

---

### Typical priority ranking

|Priority|Typical targets|
|---|---|
|⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐|Foreign governments, military, intelligence services|
|⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐|Terrorist organizations|
|⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐|State-sponsored cyber groups|
|⭐⭐⭐⭐|Nuclear and missile programs|
|⭐⭐⭐⭐|Major foreign cybercriminal organizations threatening national security|
|⭐⭐⭐|Organized transnational crime|
|⭐|Ordinary individuals with no national security relevance|

In practice, the NSA is an intelligence agency focused primarily on collecting information about **foreign governments, military organizations, intelligence services, terrorist groups, and significant cyber threats**, rather than conducting broad surveillance of the general public as an end in itself.
