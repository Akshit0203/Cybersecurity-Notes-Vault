
# Cybersecurity vs Cybercrime – Understanding the Difference

# 1. Overview

Although the terms **Cybersecurity** and **Cybercrime** are often used together, they represent **two distinct domains**.

- **Cybersecurity** focuses on **protecting** systems, networks, applications, and data from cyber threats.
    
- **Cybercrime** focuses on **investigating crimes** committed using or against digital technologies and collecting evidence for legal proceedings.
    

---

# 2. Cybersecurity

## Definition

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting information systems, networks, devices, applications, and data from unauthorized access, attacks, disruption, or damage.

## Primary Objective

- Threat Intelligence

# 3. Cybercrime

## Definition

Cybercrime is the investigation, detection, prevention, and prosecution of crimes involving computers, networks, digital devices, or the internet.

## Primary Objective

**Identify offenders, collect admissible evidence, and support legal prosecution.**

## Typical Activities

- Digital Forensics
    
- Mobile Forensics
    
- Computer Forensics
    
- Network Forensics
    
- Cloud Forensics
    
- Cryptocurrency Investigations

- Dark Web Investigations
    
- Call Detail Record (CDR) Analysis
    
- IPDR Analysis
    
- Financial Fraud Investigation
    
- Malware Attribution
    
- Chain of Custody Management
    
- Court Reporting & Expert Testimony
    

## Common Roles

# 4. Cybersecurity vs Cybercrime

|Cybersecurity|Cybercrime Investigation|
|---|---|
|Prevent attacks|Investigate attacks after they occur|
|Protect systems|Collect digital evidence|
|Reduce cyber risk|Identify offenders|
|Secure infrastructure|Support legal proceedings|
|Focus on defense|Focus on investigation and attribution|
|Technical security controls|Techno-legal investigation|

---

# 5. Example

## Cybersecurity Perspective

A ransomware attack occurs.

Security team responsibilities:

- Detect the attack
    
- Isolate infected systems
    
- Stop lateral movement
    
- Restore from backups
    
- Patch vulnerabilities
    
- Strengthen defenses
    

---

## Cybercrime Perspective

Investigators examine:

- Who launched the attack?
    
- How was access obtained?
    
- Which systems were affected?
    
- What evidence exists?
    
- Can the attackers be identified?
    
- Can evidence be presented in court?

# 8. Techno-Legal Nature of Cybercrime

Unlike cybersecurity, cybercrime investigation is a **techno-legal discipline**.

An investigator must not only identify evidence but also:

- Preserve its integrity.
    
- Maintain the chain of custody.
    
- Document forensic methods.
    
- Explain how conclusions were reached.
    
- Present findings that are legally admissible.
    

---

# 9. Key Takeaways

- **Cybersecurity** is about **preventing, detecting, and responding to cyber threats** to protect systems and data.
    
- **Cybercrime Investigation** is about **collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence** to identify offenders and support legal action.
    
- Cybersecurity is primarily a **defensive engineering discipline**, while cybercrime investigation is a **techno-legal investigative discipline**.
    
- Although both fields share technical foundations, they require different skill sets, tools, objectives, and career paths.
