# CSAM Perpetrator Research Report (2026)

## Report Overview

The **CSAM Perpetrator Research Report (2026)**, developed by **Protect Children** and sponsored by **Ofcom**, provides an in-depth analysis of how individuals who engage with Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) interact with modern digital environments. The report focuses on **offender behaviour, technological pathways, and platform ecosystems**, rather than victim experiences, to better understand how abuse is facilitated online.

Its primary goal is to support **evidence-based policymaking, platform safety improvements, and law enforcement strategies** by identifying patterns in how perpetrators discover, access, and distribute CSAM. The report also highlights the growing role of **artificial intelligence (AI)** and emerging technologies in shaping these behaviours.

---

# Research Objectives

The study aims to:

- Examine **initial exposure pathways**, including accidental and intentional encounters with CSAM.
    
- Identify the **types of platforms and services** used to access and distribute illegal content.
    
- Analyze how **platform design features** (e.g., anonymity, moderation, recommendation systems) influence offending behaviour.
    
- Evaluate the **impact of AI and emerging technologies** on accessibility and content creation.
    
- Assess the effectiveness of **deterrence strategies and behavioural interventions**.
    
- Provide **actionable recommendations** for governments, regulators, and technology companies to enhance child protection online.
    

---

# Research Methodology

- Conducted as a **global anonymous online survey** targeting individuals who admitted to searching for or viewing CSAM.
    
- Total respondents: **20,592 adults** from multiple regions worldwide.
    
- Survey distributed via the **Ahmia search engine**, which indexes dark web (.onion) sites, allowing access to a population that may otherwise be difficult to reach.
    
- Included **54 structured questions** covering:
    
    - Behavioural patterns
        
    - Platform usage
        
    - Technology preferences
        
    - AI awareness and usage
        
    - Deterrence experiences
        
- Available in **24 languages**, ensuring broad international participation.
    
- Ethical considerations included anonymity and voluntary participation.
    
- After completion, participants were directed to **support and prevention resources**, such as:
    
    - **ReDirection**
        
    - **Stop It Now**
        
    - **Help Wanted**

# Key Findings

## 1. Early Exposure

- A significant proportion of respondents reported **early exposure to pornography**, often preceding exposure to CSAM.
    
- Nearly **half of respondents encountered CSAM unintentionally**, often through:
    
    - Misleading links
        
    - Search engine results
        
    - Peer sharing
        
- Over time, some individuals transitioned from accidental exposure to **intentional searching**.
    
- Many respondents began engaging with CSAM **before reaching adulthood**, highlighting adolescence as a **critical intervention window**.
    
- Early exposure combined with curiosity, lack of awareness, and accessibility contributes to behavioural escalation.
    

---

## 2. Platform Usage

Respondents reported using a wide range of digital platforms, demonstrating that CSAM distribution is **multi-platform and decentralized**:

- **Open web search engines** (e.g., mainstream search platforms)
    
- **Dark web search engines** (e.g., Ahmia)
    
- **Adult content websites**
    
- **Social media platforms**
    
- **Encrypted messaging applications**
    
- **Peer-to-peer and file-sharing services**
    

Key insight:

- CSAM is **not limited to hidden or dark web environments**; it exists across both mainstream and hidden networks.
    
- Offenders often use **multiple platforms simultaneously**, switching between them based on accessibility, anonymity, and risk.
    

---

## 3. Platform Design & Online Safety

The report emphasizes that **platform architecture and design choices directly influence misuse**.

Key risk factors include:

- Weak moderation systems
    
- Lack of identity verification
    
- Poor reporting mechanisms
    
- Algorithmic recommendation systems that may unintentionally surface harmful content
    

Protective measures identified:

- **Content moderation systems** (automated + human review)
    
- **Age assurance technologies** to restrict access
    
- **Identity verification mechanisms** to reduce anonymity abuse
    
- **Perceptual hash matching** to detect known illegal content
    
- **URL detection systems** to block harmful links
    
- **Efficient reporting and takedown processes**
    
- **Strong enforcement policies** (account suspension, bans)
    

Conclusion:

- Well-designed platforms can **significantly reduce opportunities for abuse**, while poorly designed systems may unintentionally facilitate it.
    

---

## 4. Artificial Intelligence

AI is identified as a **rapidly evolving risk factor** in the CSAM ecosystem.

Key concerns:

- **AI-generated illegal imagery**, including synthetic content that mimics real victims
    
- Lower barriers to content creation using **generative AI tools**
    
- Difficulty in distinguishing **real vs. synthetic content**, complicating detection and legal classification
    
- Potential misuse of AI for **content discovery, recommendation, or evasion of detection systems**
    

Recommendations:

- Implement **AI safety frameworks and governance policies**
    
- Develop **advanced detection tools** for synthetic content
    
- Ensure **responsible deployment of generative AI technologies**
    
- Increase collaboration between AI developers and safety organizations
    

---

## 5. Emerging Technologies

The report highlights several technologies that may reshape offender behaviour:

- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Extended Reality (XR)**:
    
    - Potential for immersive environments that could be misused
        
- **Encrypted communication services**:
    
    - Increased privacy but reduced visibility for law enforcement
        
- **VPN usage**:
    
    - Enables anonymity and geographic masking
        
- **Cloud storage platforms**:
    
    - Facilitate storage and sharing of large volumes of content
        
- **High-speed internet and mobile access**:
    
    - Increase accessibility and distribution speed
        

Implication:

- As technology evolves, **traditional detection and enforcement methods must adapt** to new digital environments.
    

---

## 6. Deterrence & Prevention

The report evaluates various **intervention strategies** aimed at reducing offending behaviour:

- **Warning messages** displayed during searches
    
- **Prevention-focused websites** offering support and education
    
- **Behavioural nudges** that interrupt harmful actions
    
- **Platform bans and account restrictions**
    
- **Public awareness campaigns**
    

Findings:

- Some respondents reported that these interventions:
    
    - Increased awareness of harm
        
    - Encouraged reconsideration of behaviour
        
    - Motivated seeking help or support
        

Conclusion:

- Early and targeted interventions can be **effective in disrupting harmful behaviour patterns**.
    

---

## 7. Intersecting Harms

The report identifies that CSAM often exists alongside other forms of harmful content, such as:

- Violent material
    
- Exploitative content
    
- Extremist content
    

Implication:

- Platforms should adopt a **holistic safety approach**, addressing multiple types of harm simultaneously rather than treating CSAM in isolation.
    

---

# Cybersecurity & Platform Security Lessons

From a cybersecurity perspective, the report highlights:

- Security strategies must integrate **prevention, detection, and response mechanisms**.
    
- **Platform architecture** plays a critical role in enabling or restricting misuse.
    
- **AI systems require dedicated security controls** to prevent exploitation.
    
- **Trust & Safety teams** are essential for monitoring and enforcement.
    
- **Identity verification and age assurance** reduce anonymity-related risks.
    
- **Content detection technologies** (e.g., perceptual hashing) are critical for identifying known illegal material.
    
- **Continuous monitoring and proactive moderation** are more effective than reactive approaches.

- **Network traffic logs**
    
- **Account linkage and correlation data**
    
- **Hash databases for known CSAM detection**
    

---

# Key Recommendations

The report recommends that stakeholders take the following actions:

- Strengthen **online safety regulations and enforcement frameworks**.
    
- Improve **platform moderation systems**, combining automation and human oversight.
    
- Deploy **AI safety mechanisms** to prevent misuse of generative technologies.
    
- Expand **age assurance and identity verification systems**.
    
- Enhance detection of **previously unknown CSAM**, not just known hashed content.
    
- Foster **collaboration between technology companies, researchers, and law enforcement agencies**.
    
- Continue **long-term research on offender behaviour** to inform prevention strategies.
    

---

# Final Conclusion

The report concludes that **digital platforms, emerging technologies, and artificial intelligence are central factors influencing how CSAM is accessed and distributed**. Offender behaviour is shaped by accessibility, anonymity, and platform design.

Effective prevention requires a **multi-layered approach**, including:

- Technical safeguards
    
- Strong platform governance
    
- Behavioural interventions
    
- AI safety controls
    
- International cooperation
    

Ultimately, understanding offender behaviour is essential for **designing safer digital ecosystems and strengthening global efforts to protect children online**.
