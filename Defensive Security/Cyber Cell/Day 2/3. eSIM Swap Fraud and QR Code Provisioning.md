
# eSIM Swap Fraud and QR Code Provisioning

## What Is an eSIM?

eSIM stands for:

**Embedded SIM**

Unlike traditional SIM cards:

- No physical SIM is inserted.
    
- The SIM chip is permanently embedded inside the device.
    
- Mobile operator profiles are downloaded digitally.
    

Examples:

- iPhone
    
- Samsung Galaxy
    
- Google Pixel
    

---

## How eSIM Activation Works

Normally:

1. Customer requests eSIM.
    
2. Telecom operator generates an activation profile.
    
3. Operator provides a QR code.
    
4. User scans the QR code.
    
5. Mobile profile downloads to the device.
    
6. Network activates the eSIM.
    

---

## Why eSIM Is Attractive to Attackers

Traditional SIM Swap:

- Requires physical SIM replacement.
    

eSIM Swap:

- Can be performed remotely if the attacker successfully impersonates the victim.
    

This makes eSIM-related fraud a growing concern.

---

## Typical eSIM Swap Attack Chain

### Step 1 – Information Gathering

Attacker collects:

- Mobile number
    
- Name
    
- Email address
    
- Aadhaar/PAN details
    
- Banking information
    

Using:

- Data breaches
    
- Phishing
    
- Social engineering
    
- SIM registration leaks
    

---

### Step 2 – Social Engineering

Attacker contacts:

- Telecom provider
    
- Customer support
    

Claims:

> "I lost my phone and need an eSIM activation."

---

### Step 3 – eSIM QR Code Issued

If verification procedures fail:

The telecom operator may issue:

- eSIM activation QR code
    

to the attacker-controlled email or device.

---

### Step 4 – QR Code Scanned

Attacker scans:

- eSIM QR code
    

The mobile number is transferred.

---

### Step 5 – Victim Loses Network Access

Victim suddenly notices:

- No signal
    
- No calls
    
- No SMS
    
- No OTPs
    

The attacker now controls the number.

---

### Step 6 – Account Takeover

The attacker uses:

- OTP interception
    
- Password resets
    

to access:

- Banking apps
    
- UPI accounts
    
- Email accounts
    
- Social media accounts
    

---

## Investigation Indicators

Victim reports:

### Sudden Loss of Signal

Common symptom:

> "My SIM suddenly stopped working."

---

### Unauthorized eSIM Activation

Telecom logs show:

- New eSIM profile issued
    
- QR code generated
    
- Profile downloaded
    

---

### OTP-Based Fraud

Investigators often find:

- Bank OTPs intercepted
    
- Account recovery requests
    
- Password resets
    

after the SIM transfer.

---

## Digital Evidence

Investigators examine:

### Telecom Records

- SIM swap requests
    
- eSIM activation logs
    
- Customer service interactions
    

### Email Records

- QR code delivery emails
    
- Activation messages
    

### IP Logs

- Source IPs used during activation
    

### Device Information

- New device registration
    
- IMEI information
    

---

## Prevention

### Enable Strong Account Security

Use:

- MFA
    
- Strong passwords
    

---

### Monitor Signal Loss

Unexpected loss of network service can be a warning sign.

---

### Secure Email Accounts

Many eSIM activation links and QR codes are delivered through email.

---

### Contact Telecom Provider Immediately

If:

- Signal disappears unexpectedly
    
- SIM suddenly stops working
    

verify whether a SIM swap or eSIM activation occurred.

---

## Key Takeaway

An eSIM swap attack does not require physical access to a SIM card. If an attacker successfully convinces a telecom provider to issue an eSIM activation profile, they can scan the QR code, transfer the victim's mobile number to their own device, intercept OTPs, and potentially take over banking, email, and social media accounts. In many investigations, the QR code itself is not the vulnerability—the real weakness is identity verification failure during the eSIM issuance process.
