## Myth: "I can turn off my location and become untraceable."

Not entirely true.

### 1. Your IP Address Reveals Approximate Location

Whenever you connect to the internet:

- Your device sends traffic through an IP address.
    
- Websites, apps, and service providers can see that IP.
    
- An IP address can usually reveal:
    
    - Country
        
    - State/Region
        
    - City (approximate)
        
    - ISP/Network Provider
        

Therefore, disabling GPS does not prevent all forms of location tracking.

---

### 2. GPS Off ≠ Location Hidden

A device's location can be estimated using:

- GPS satellites
    
- Cellular towers
    
- Wi-Fi networks
    
- Bluetooth beacons
    
- IP address information
    

Turning off one source does not necessarily disable the others.

---

### 3. Understanding IPDR (Internet Protocol Detail Records)

IPDR stands for **Internet Protocol Detail Records**.

Internet Service Providers (ISPs) and telecom operators may maintain records that can include:

- Assigned IP addresses
    
- Date and time of internet sessions
    
- Subscriber/account information
    
- Network usage details
    
- Connection logs
    

These records can help correlate:

- Which subscriber was using a particular IP address at a specific time
    
- When a device connected to the internet
    
- Which network was used
    

IPDRs generally do not provide precise GPS coordinates, but they can be useful for identifying internet activity and associating it with a subscriber account.

---

### 4. iPhone Tracking Through Apple's Find My Network

If an iPhone is powered off but still has battery remaining:

- Newer iPhones can continue broadcasting Bluetooth signals.
    
- Nearby Apple devices (iPhones, iPads, Macs) can detect these signals.
    
- The location is securely relayed to Apple's Find My network.
    
- The owner can view the device location through iCloud/Find My.
    

This is similar to how AirTags work.

How much battery does "Find My when powered off" use?

Apple does not publicly disclose the exact battery consumption in mAh for the **Find My when powered off** feature.

However, based on hardware analysis and battery behavior:
#### Rough Estimate

When an iPhone is powered off but still discoverable:

- A small low-power chip remains active.
- Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) beacons are transmitted periodically.
- Power consumption is extremely low compared to normal phone operation.

For a modern iPhone with a battery around **3,000–4,500 mAh**:

- The Find My reserve likely consumes only a few mAh per day.
- Estimates from researchers and teardown analyses suggest it is probably in the range of **1–10 mAh/day**, depending on model and conditions.
#### Practical Observation

Apple states that:

> An iPhone can remain findable for several hours, and sometimes much longer, after being powered off.

Users have reported:

- Find My still working overnight.
- Devices remaining discoverable for many hours after shutdown.
- In some cases, over 24 hours if the battery was not already low.

#### Why So Little Power?

The phone is **not running iOS normally**.

Only a few components stay active:

- Bluetooth controller
- Secure Element
- Ultra-low-power controller

The:

- Screen
- CPU cores
- Cellular modem
- Wi-Fi subsystem

are mostly inactive.

---

### 5. What If There Are No Nearby Apple Devices?

Find My Network requires another Apple device nearby to relay the location.

If no Apple device is nearby:

- The location may not update.
    
- The phone's last known location may still be visible.
    

---

### 6. What If the Battery Is Completely Dead?

If the battery is fully depleted:

- No Bluetooth transmission.
    
- No GPS.
    
- No cellular communication.
    
- No Wi-Fi communication.
    

The device cannot actively report its location.

Only the last known location before power loss may be available.

---

### 7. Can Law Enforcement Still Trace a Device?

Sometimes yes, depending on circumstances.

Possible sources include:

- Historical cell tower records
    
- IPDR (Internet Protocol Detail Records)
    
- Wi-Fi connection logs
    
- Cloud account activity
    
- ISP records
    
- App telemetry
    
- Previously recorded location history
    

These records can help investigators determine where a device was, which networks it used, and what internet activity was associated with it before it went offline.

---

## Key Takeaway

"Location Off" does not mean "untraceable."

As long as a device is powered and communicating with networks, multiple sources of location information may exist. GPS is only one method of determining location. IP addresses, cellular networks, Wi-Fi systems, Bluetooth-based tracking networks, and records such as IPDRs may all contribute to identifying a device's activity or approximate location. A completely powered-down device with a dead battery cannot actively transmit its current location, but historical records may still exist elsewhere.
