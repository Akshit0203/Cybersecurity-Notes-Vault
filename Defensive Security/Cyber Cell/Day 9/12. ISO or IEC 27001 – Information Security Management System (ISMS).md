
# ISO/IEC 27001 – Information Security Management System (ISMS)

# 1. What is ISO/IEC 27001?

**ISO/IEC 27001** is the world's leading international standard for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**.

Published by:

- **ISO** – International Organization for Standardization
    
- **IEC** – International Electrotechnical Commission
    

Current version:

- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**
    

---

# 2. What is an ISMS?

**ISMS (Information Security Management System)** is a systematic framework of:

- Policies
    
- Processes
    
- Controls
    
- Procedures
    
- Technologies
    
- People
    

designed to manage information security risks.

---

# 3. Objectives of ISO 27001

The standard aims to protect:

- Confidentiality
    
- Integrity
    
- Availability
    

Also known as the **CIA Triad**.

```text
Information Security

        CIA

Confidentiality
      │
Integrity
      │
Availability
```

---

# 4. CIA Triad

## Confidentiality

Ensure only authorized individuals can access information.

Examples:

- Encryption
    
- Access control
    
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
    

---

## Integrity

Ensure information is accurate and has not been altered without authorization.

Examples:

- Hashing
    
- Digital signatures
    
- File integrity monitoring
    

---

## Availability

Ensure systems and data are available when needed.

Examples:

- Backups
    
- Disaster Recovery
    
- High Availability
    
- Redundant servers
    

---

# 5. ISO 27001 Risk-Based Approach

The standard follows a **risk management methodology**.

```text
Identify Assets

↓

Identify Threats

↓

Identify Vulnerabilities

↓

Risk Assessment

↓

Risk Treatment

↓

Implement Controls

↓

Continuous Monitoring
```

---

# 6. Information Security Management Cycle

ISO 27001 follows the **Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA)** cycle.

```text
PLAN

↓

DO

↓

CHECK

↓

ACT
```

This enables continual improvement.

# 8. ISO 27001 Controls (Annex A)

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 includes **93 security controls**, organized into four themes:

|Theme|Examples|
|---|---|
|Organizational Controls|Policies, roles, supplier security|
|People Controls|Awareness, background checks|
|Physical Controls|CCTV, access cards, secure facilities|
|Technological Controls|Firewalls, encryption, logging, MFA|

These controls are selected based on the organization's risk assessment.

---

# 10. Statement of Applicability (SoA)

One of the most important ISO 27001 documents.

The SoA records:

- Which Annex A controls are applicable
    
- Which controls are excluded
    
- Why they are included or excluded
    
- Implementation status
    

---

# 11. Internal Audit

Organizations perform periodic audits to verify:

- Policies are followed.
    
- Controls are effective.
    
- Risks are managed.
    
- Compliance is maintained.
    

---

# 12. External Certification Audit

Certification bodies conduct independent audits in two stages:

### Stage 1

- Documentation review
    
- Readiness assessment
    

### Stage 2

- Practical implementation review
    
- Employee interviews
    
- Evidence verification
    
- Control testing
    

Successful organizations receive ISO/IEC 27001 certification.

# 19. Key Takeaways

- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** is the international standard for building and maintaining an **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**.
    
- It follows a **risk-based approach** and the **Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA)** continuous improvement cycle.
    
- The standard aims to protect the **Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA)** of information.
    
- Organizations select appropriate controls from **Annex A's 93 security controls** based on their risk assessment.
    
- Certification demonstrates that an organization has implemented a structured and internationally recognized information security management system, but it does **not** guarantee that the organization is free from cyberattacks.
