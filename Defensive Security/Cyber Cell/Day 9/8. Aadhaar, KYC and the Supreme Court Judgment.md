
# Aadhaar, KYC and the Supreme Court Judgment

# 1. Aadhaar and the Supreme Court (2018)

In **2018**, the Supreme Court of India delivered its landmark judgment in the **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Aadhaar) case**.

The Court held that:

- Aadhaar is **constitutionally valid**.
    
- Aadhaar **cannot generally be made mandatory by private companies** solely because they demand it.
    
- Aadhaar remains mandatory where specifically authorized by law (for certain government welfare schemes and other statutory uses).
    

The judgment recognized the **Right to Privacy** as a fundamental right while balancing the legitimate use of Aadhaar.

---

# 2. Justice K.S. Puttaswamy Case

Official Name:

**Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India (2018)**

Major principles:

- Privacy is a Fundamental Right.
    
- Aadhaar use must have a lawful basis.
    
- Proportionality must be maintained.
    
- Data protection is essential.
    

---

# 3. Aadhaar Act Amendments

The Aadhaar Act has been amended over time to regulate authentication and voluntary use.

Current rules allow Aadhaar to be used in many situations **where permitted by law**, but organizations cannot simply demand Aadhaar without an applicable legal basis.

---

# 4. Is Aadhaar Mandatory Everywhere?

**No.**

It is **not universally mandatory**.

Whether Aadhaar is required depends on the applicable law or regulator.

Examples:

|Service|Aadhaar Mandatory?|
|---|---|
|Government welfare schemes (where prescribed by law)|Often Yes|
|SIM verification|Aadhaar is **not mandatory**; other officially valid documents can be used.|
|Bank KYC|Aadhaar is generally **optional**; other officially valid documents are accepted.|
|College admissions|Usually No (unless required under a specific government rule)|
|Insurance KYC|Aadhaar is **not the only acceptable document**.|

---

# 5. How Can KYC Be Completed Without Aadhaar?

Most regulated entities accept other **Officially Valid Documents (OVDs)**.

Examples:

- Passport
    
- Driving Licence
    
- Voter ID (EPIC)
    
- NREGA Job Card
    
- Aadhaar (where voluntarily provided or legally applicable)
    

The exact list depends on the regulator (e.g., RBI, SEBI, IRDAI).

---

# 6. Masked Aadhaar

A **Masked Aadhaar** hides the first eight digits of the Aadhaar number.

Example:

```text
XXXX XXXX 1234
```

Benefits:

- Reduces unnecessary exposure of the Aadhaar number.
    
- Useful where identity proof is required but the full Aadhaar number is not.
    

---

# 7. Virtual ID (VID)

A **Virtual ID (VID)** is a temporary, revocable **16-digit random number** generated by UIDAI.

Features:

- Can be used instead of sharing the Aadhaar number for many authentication purposes.
    
- Helps protect the actual Aadhaar number.
    
- Can be regenerated by the Aadhaar holder.
    

---

# 8. Aadhaar Enrolment ID (EID)

The **Enrolment ID (EID)** is issued when a person enrolls for Aadhaar.

It consists of:

- Enrolment Number
    
- Date
    
- Time
    

It is mainly used to track Aadhaar generation or update status.

---

# 9. Aadhaar PDF Password

Downloaded Aadhaar PDFs are password protected.

Password format:

```text
First 4 letters of the name (CAPITALS)
+
Year of Birth
```

Example:

```text
Name: Rahul Sharma

Year: 1998

Password:

RAHU1998
```

---

# 10. Best Privacy Practices

- Share Aadhaar only when legally required.
    
- Prefer **Masked Aadhaar** where acceptable.
    
- Use **Virtual ID (VID)** when supported.
    
- Avoid sharing photocopies unnecessarily.
    
- Verify why an organization is requesting identity documents.
    

---

# 11. Key Takeaways

- The **2018 Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Aadhaar)** judgment upheld Aadhaar but limited mandatory use by private entities.
    
- Aadhaar is **not universally mandatory** for services such as SIM cards, banking, insurance, or education; other officially valid documents are often accepted, subject to the applicable regulator's rules.
    
- **Masked Aadhaar** and **Virtual ID (VID)** are privacy-enhancing alternatives that reduce exposure of the full Aadhaar number.
    
- Common alternatives for KYC include **Passport**, **Driving Licence**, and **Voter ID**, depending on the regulatory requirements.
