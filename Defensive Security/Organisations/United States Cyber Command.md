# United States Cyber Command (USCYBERCOM)

> **Type:** Unified Combatant Command & Cyber Force — US Department of Defense
> **Founded:** 21 May 2010 (as sub-unified command) | **Elevated:** 2018 (full unified combatant command)
> **Headquarters:** Fort George G. Meade, Maryland, USA
> **Part of:** US Department of Defense
> **Nicknames:** USCYBERCOM, CYBERCOM
> **Website:** [cybercom.mil](https://www.cybercom.mil/)

---

## What It Does

USCYBERCOM is one of the **eleven unified combatant commands** of the US Department of Defense. It is responsible for:

- **Directing cyberspace operations** — plans, coordinates, and conducts full-spectrum military cyber operations
- **Defending DoD networks** — secures the .mil domain and all Department of Defense information networks
- **Offensive cyber operations** — conducts cyberattacks against adversaries to enable actions in all domains
- **Cyber intelligence** — global hunt operations on foreign networks to identify malicious activity and vulnerabilities
- **Election defence** — protecting US elections from foreign cyber interference
- **Counter-ransomware** — operations against ransomware threat actors
- **Combating foreign cyber actors** — persistent engagement against nation-state and terrorist cyber threats
- **Supporting national security operations** — providing cyber capabilities to other combatant commands

### Mission Statement
> *"USCYBERCOM plans, coordinates, integrates, synchronizes and conducts activities to: direct the operations and defense of specified Department of Defense information networks and; prepare to, and when directed, conduct full spectrum military cyberspace operations in order to enable actions in all domains, ensure US/Allied freedom of action in cyberspace and deny the same to our adversaries."*

The MD5 hash of this statement (`9ec4c12949a4f31474f299058ce2b22a`) is embedded in the command's official emblem.

## Organisational Structure

### Cyber Teams (133 teams since 2015)
| Team Type | Count | Mission |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **National Mission Teams** | 13 | Defend against broad cyberattacks on the nation |
| **Cyber Protection Teams** | 68 | Defend priority DoD networks and systems |
| **Combat Mission Teams** | 27 | Integrated cyberspace attacks supporting operational plans |
| **Cyber Support Teams** | 25 | Analytic and planning support |

### Service Cyber Components
| Component | Acronym | HQ |
|-----------|---------|-----|
| **US Army Cyber Command** | ARCYBER | Fort Gordon, Georgia |
| **Marine Corps Forces Cyberspace Command** | MARFORCYBER | Fort Meade, Maryland |
| **US Fleet Cyber Command / Tenth Fleet** | FLTCYBER / 10F | Fort Meade, Maryland |
| **Air Forces Cyber / Sixteenth Air Force** | AFCYBER / 16AF | JBSA-Lackland, Texas |

### Sub-Unified Commands

**Cyber National Mission Force (CNMF)**
- Established: Jan 2014 | Elevated to sub-unified command: Dec 2022
- 39 joint cyber teams, 2,000+ personnel
- Missions: Election defence, counter-ransomware, global hunt operations, combating foreign cyber actors
- Hunt operations conducted in **18 countries** across **50+ foreign networks** since 2018

**Department of Defense Cyber Defense Command (DCDC)**
- Originally: Joint Force Headquarters-DoD Information Network (JFHQ-DoDIN)
- IOC: Jan 2015 | FOC: Jan 2018
- Secures, operates, and defends the DoD Information Network (DoDIN)

**Joint Task Force Ares (JTF-ARES)**
- Established: 2016 to combat ISIS cyber threats
- Now shifted focus to **nation-state actors** (primarily Indo-Pacific region)

## History

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| **Oct 2006** | US Air Force announces intent to create a cyber command |
| **23 Jun 2009** | USCYBERCOM created under US Strategic Command at the NSA headquarters |
| **21 May 2010** | Initial Operational Capability (IOC) achieved |
| **31 Oct 2010** | Full Operational Capability (FOC) achieved |
| **2014** | Cyber National Mission Force activated |
| **Dec 2016** | NDAA FY2017 elevates USCYBERCOM to unified combatant command |
| **Aug 2017** | Elevation to full independent unified combatant command announced |
| **2018** | Global hunt operations begin under "persistent engagement" strategy |
| **Dec 2022** | CNMF elevated to sub-unified command |
| **Feb 2025** | US government pauses offensive cyber operations against Russia |

## Key Relationships

- **National Security Agency (NSA)** — co-located at Fort Meade; the CYBERCOM commander has historically also served as NSA Director (dual-hat arrangement)
- **Department of Homeland Security (DHS)** — responsible for civilian (.gov) network defence; USCYBERCOM handles military (.mil) networks
- **Defense Information Systems Agency (DISA)** — provides technical and network assistance to USCYBERCOM

---

## International Impact

USCYBERCOM's creation prompted cyber force buildups globally:
- **South Korea** — created cyber warfare command (Dec 2009) in response to North Korea's cyber unit
- **UK (GCHQ)** — began preparing a dedicated cyber force
- **China** — introduced a department for defensive cyber war and information security (2010)
