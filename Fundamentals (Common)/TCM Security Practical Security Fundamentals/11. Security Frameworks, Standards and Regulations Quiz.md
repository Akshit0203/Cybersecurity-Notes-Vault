# Security Frameworks, Standards and Regulations — Notes & Check-In Quiz

## Popular Security Frameworks

### NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

- **Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
- **Purpose:** Improve cybersecurity posture across organizations of all sizes
- **Details:** Originally developed for critical infrastructure, now a general-purpose model. Organised around **five core functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover**. Highly adaptable — popular from small business to large enterprise.

### COBIT (Control Objectives for Information Related Technologies)

- **Publisher:** ISACA
- **Purpose:** Governance and management of enterprise IT
- **Details:** Broader in scope than pure cybersecurity — focuses on aligning IT strategies with business goals. Popular in regulated industries (finance, healthcare, government). Frequently used in audit preparation.

### CIS Controls (Center for Internet Security)

- **Publisher:** Center for Internet Security (CIS)
- **Purpose:** Prioritised, actionable security practices
- **Details:** Top 18 critical security controls grouped into three **Implementation Groups (IG1, IG2, IG3)** based on organisational maturity and size. Designed as a practical, tiered checklist for small to medium sized organisations.

## Important Security Standards

### NIST SP 800-53

- **Publisher:** NIST
- **Purpose:** Catalogue of security and privacy controls for federal systems
- **Details:** ~900 controls spanning access control, auditing, configuration management, incident response. Required for government agencies and contractors handling sensitive/classified data. Also used by private sector as a framework.

### ISO/IEC 27001

- **Publisher:** International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
- **Purpose:** Requirements for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS)
- **Details:** International standard with a top-down, risk-based approach involving leadership and continuous improvement. Can be formally certified by third-party auditor, but many companies use it as a framework without certification.

### PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)

- **Publisher:** PCI Security Standards Council (Mastercard, Visa, etc.)
- **Purpose:** Protect credit cardholder data and reduce credit card fraud
- **Details:** 12 specific requirements including strong access controls, network monitoring/testing, firewalls, encryption, EDR, and information security policy. **Required for any company processing/storing credit card data.**

### SOC 2 (System and Organization Controls)

- **Publisher:** AICPA (American Institute of Certified Public Accountants)
- **Purpose:** Evaluate security controls via the five Trust Services Criteria
- **Details:** The five criteria are: **Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy**. Not a certification — an independent auditor produces a SOC 2 report that can be presented to clients to evaluate security/reliability.

## Important Security Regulations

### HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)

- **Governing Body:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)
- **Purpose:** Safeguard Protected Health Information (PHI)
- **Details:** U.S. federal regulation mandating protection of electronic health data. Required for healthcare providers, insurance companies, and their contractors. Non-compliance → severe fines and reputational damage.

### GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

- **Governing Body:** European Data Protection Board (EDPB)
- **Purpose:** Protect personal data and privacy rights of EU individuals
- **Details:** EU regulation enforced **globally** for any organisation processing EU citizens' data, **regardless of where the data is stored**. Requirements include breach notification, consent, transparency, and data minimisation. Violations → fines in tens of millions of euros.

### FISMA (Federal Information Security Management Act)

- **Governing Body:** Office of Management and Budget + NIST
- **Purpose:** Ensure U.S. federal agencies implement security controls
- **Details:** Mandates use of NIST standards (e.g., SP 800-53). Applies to government agencies **and** private companies/contractors working with government.

### SOX (Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002)

- **Governing Body:** U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)
- **Purpose:** Improve accuracy, transparency, and reliability of corporate financial reporting
- **Details:** U.S. federal law passed in response to corporate accounting scandals. Requires controls like proper access controls, change management, and detailed logging of systems impacting financial reporting. Failure to comply → financial **and criminal** penalties for executives.

## Quick Comparison Table

| Name | Type | Key Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **NIST CSF** | Framework | Broad cybersecurity (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover) |
| **COBIT** | Framework | IT governance aligned with business goals |
| **CIS Controls** | Framework | Practical, prioritised top-18 security controls |
| **NIST SP 800-53** | Standard | ~900 security controls for federal systems |
| **ISO 27001** | Standard | ISMS requirements (international) |
| **PCI DSS** | Standard | Credit card data protection |
| **SOC 2** | Standard | Trust services criteria audit |
| **HIPAA** | Regulation | Health data (U.S.) |
| **GDPR** | Regulation | Personal data privacy (EU, global reach) |
| **FISMA** | Regulation | Federal information security (U.S.) |
| **SOX** | Regulation | Financial reporting integrity (U.S.) |

> **Framework** = voluntary guidelines, adaptable to needs
> **Standard** = specific requirements (may require certification/audit)
> **Regulation** = law — non-compliance results in penalties

## Check-In Quiz

> [!question] Q1: Your organization is planning to start processing credit card payments, which standard will you need to comply with?
> - [ ] FISMA
> - [ ] NIST SP 800-53
> - [ ] OWASP Top 10
> - [ ] PCI DSS
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 
> **PCI DSS**
> </details>

> [!question] Q2: Which of the following is an example of an administrative detective control?
> - [ ] Routine and scheduled audits of data access logs
> - [ ] Implementing MFA on all systems
> - [ ] Installing a firewall
> - [ ] Requiring a debrief after an incident
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 
> **Routine and scheduled audits of data access logs**
> </details>

> [!question] Q3: You work at a small organization that previously did not have a cybersecurity team or policy. A small budget has been allocated, which framework would be a good starting point for this organization?
> - [ ] NIST CSF
> - [ ] COBIT
> - [ ] CIS Controls
> - [ ] FISMA
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 
> **CIS Controls**
> 
> CIS Controls provide a practical, prioritised set of cybersecurity actions — ideal for building cybersecurity from scratch with limited resources.
> </details>

> [!question] Q4: Your organization has users in the EU that they are planning to collect data on, however, they are planning on storing this data only on North American servers, which of the following is correct?
> - [ ] Since the data is stored outside of the EU the organization does not need to comply with GDPR
> - [ ] Since the organization is storing data in North America it must comply with FISMA
> - [ ] The organization does not need to comply with FISMA or GDPR as they are both voluntary frameworks
> - [ ] The organization must comply with GDPR if they are collecting EU citizens' data regardless of where the data is stored
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 
> **The organization must comply with GDPR if they are collecting EU citizens' data regardless of where the data is stored**
> </details>

> [!question] Q5: An organization is using the NIST CSF and decides not to implement controls for one of the core functions, which of the following is true?
> - [ ] Since NIST CSF is a framework, organizations are free to modify them to fit their needs
> - [ ] Since NIST CSF is a standard, strict adherence to guidelines is required
> - [ ] Since NIST CSF is a U.S. regulation, failure to comply could result in penalties such as fines
> - [ ] Since NIST CSF is a framework, organizations should strictly follow all guidelines
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 
> **Since NIST CSF is a framework, organizations are free to modify them to fit their needs**
> </details>
