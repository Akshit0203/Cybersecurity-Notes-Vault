# Introduction to Information Gathering

> Information gathering (a.k.a. **reconnaissance**) is the first and arguably most important phase of any penetration test. Every subsequent phase — enumeration, exploitation, post-exploitation — depends on the intelligence collected here.

---
## What Is Information Gathering?

- The process of **collecting information** about a target individual, company, website, or system.
- The **more information** you have on your target, the **more successful** the latter stages of the pentest will be.
- Broken down into two types: **Passive** and **Active** information gathering.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> "Information gathering" and "reconnaissance" are used interchangeably — they refer to the same process.

---
## Why Is It So Important?

Information gathered in this phase directly fuels exploitation strategies. Two real-world examples:

### Example 1 — Website Pentest
A company hires you to test their website. During information gathering, your objective is to:
- Identify **web technologies** used on the site
- Find the **IP address** of the server hosting the website
- Discover potential **vulnerabilities**
- Build a clear picture of what you're targeting before touching anything

### Example 2 — Corporate Network Pentest
A company gives you a scope that includes public-facing assets (e.g., a web server) and internal systems. Information gathering opens multiple attack paths:

1. **Direct exploitation** — Learn about the web server → find a vulnerability → exploit it → pivot into the internal network.
2. **Phishing** — Identify employee **names and email addresses** during recon → craft a phishing email to steal credentials or send a malicious attachment (e.g., a Word/Excel macro) → gain access to an internal workstation → pivot into the internal network.

> [!TIP]
> The key takeaway: the more you know about your target, the more attack vectors you can identify. Without thorough recon, you may be left with no clear path into the target's digital infrastructure.

---
## Passive vs. Active Information Gathering

| Aspect                 | Passive                                                              | Active                                                                |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**         | Gathering information **without** actively engaging with the target  | Gathering information **by** actively engaging with the target system |
| **Method**             | Using publicly available/accessible sources                          | Direct interaction — scanning, probing, querying                      |
| **Example**            | Browsing the website as an end-user, looking up WHOIS, Google dorking | Running an `nmap` port scan on the target IP                         |
| **Detection risk**     | Very low — you're using public information                           | Higher — you're communicating directly with the target                |
| **Authorization needed** | No                                                                 | **Yes** — requires written authorization                              |

> [!CAUTION]
> Active information gathering requires **authorization from the client**. Performing an `nmap` scan or any active technique on systems you do not have permission to target is **illegal** and can lead to prosecution. This is a fundamental part of the **code of ethics** of a penetration tester.

---
## What Information Are We Looking For?

### Passive Information Gathering
- Identifying **IP addresses** & **DNS** information
- Identifying **domain names** and **domain ownership** information
- Identifying **email addresses** and **social media profiles**
- Identifying **web technologies** being used on target sites
- Identifying **subdomains**

### Active Information Gathering
- Discovering **open ports** on target systems
- Learning about the **internal infrastructure** of a target network/organization
- **Enumerating** information from target systems

> [!NOTE]
> This list is not comprehensive — there is a vast amount of information you can gather in each phase. These are the most important items.
> 
> **Enumeration** (a deeper form of active information gathering) is covered in its own separate course due to its extensive scope.
