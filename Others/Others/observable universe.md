# The Observable Universe

> [!info] Core Definition
> The **observable universe** is the part of the universe from which light (or any other signal) has had enough time to reach us since the universe began.

---

## Key Facts

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Age of the universe | ~**13.8 billion years** |
| Observable universe radius | ~**46.5 billion light-years** |
| Observable universe diameter | ~**93 billion light-years** |
| Speed limit | Speed of light (Theory of Relativity) |

---

## Why Can We Observe It?

- The universe is about **13.8 billion years old**.
- Nothing can travel faster than **light** (Theory of Relativity).
- You might think we can only see objects 13.8 billion light-years away — but **space itself has been expanding** while the light was traveling, stretching the distances far beyond that.

> [!example] Analogy — The Foggy Field
> Imagine you're standing in the middle of a huge foggy field. You can only see as far as the fog allows. Beyond that, the field still exists — you just can't see it. The observable universe is your **"visible bubble."**

---

## Why 46.5 Billion Light-Years, Not 13.8?

Light has been traveling for 13.8 billion years, but **space itself has been stretching** while the light was en route. So the galaxies that emitted that light are now ~46.5 billion light-years away.

> [!example] Analogy — The Moving Walkway
> Imagine a friend starts walking toward you while you're standing on a **moving walkway** carrying both of you farther apart. Even though your friend keeps walking, the walkway makes the total distance much larger.

---

## Observable vs. Entire Universe

| | Observable Universe | Entire Universe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition** | Everything we can currently see | Everything that exists |
| **Size** | ~93 billion light-years across | Unknown — possibly **infinite** |
| **Boundary** | Limited by the speed of light & age of the universe | May extend far beyond what we can observe |

> [!important]
> The observable universe is **not the whole universe**. It's just the part whose light has reached us. The entire universe could be much larger — or even infinite.

---

## Will the Observable Universe Get Bigger?

**Yes and no.**

- ✅ As time passes, light from more distant regions reaches us → we can observe more.
- ❌ But the universe's expansion is **accelerating** → some extremely distant galaxies are receding so fast that their light will **never** reach us.

---

## Why Is Space Expanding?

> [!warning] Open Question
> We know **that** space is expanding, but we don't yet know the fundamental reason **why**.

### What We Know

- ✅ Space is expanding — strongly supported by observations.
- ✅ We can measure **how fast** it's expanding.
- ❌ We do **not** know the ultimate cause.

### Key Concepts

1. **Cosmic Inflation** — Right after the Big Bang, there was a period of extremely rapid expansion.
2. **Dark Energy** — The leading explanation for why expansion is **accelerating** today, but we don't know what dark energy actually is.

> [!example] Analogy — Raisin Bread
> Imagine a loaf of bread with raisins (galaxies) in it. As the bread bakes, the dough (space) expands — every raisin moves farther from every other raisin. The dough expands because it's baking. But for the universe, we don't yet know what's "baking" it.

---

## Does Earth Expand Too?

**No** — because **gravity is far stronger than cosmic expansion at small scales**.

### Scale Comparison

| Scale | Expansion? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Between galaxy clusters | ✅ Yes | Gravity too weak to resist expansion |
| Inside the Milky Way | ❌ No | Gravity holds stars together |
| Inside the Solar System | ❌ No | Sun's gravity holds planets in orbit |
| On Earth | ❌ No | Gravity + electromagnetic forces dominate |

> [!example] Analogy — Rubber Sheet & Bands
> Draw two dots on a stretchy rubber sheet, then tie them together with a strong rubber band. When you stretch the sheet:
> - Dots **far apart and untied** → move farther apart
> - Dots **tied together strongly** → stay the same distance (the rubber band overpowers the stretching)
>
> In the universe, the "sheet" is space and the "rubber bands" are gravity and atomic forces.

The expansion of the universe only becomes noticeable over **millions of light-years**, where there isn't enough gravity to hold everything together.

---

## Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Cosmic Expansion** | The ongoing increase in distances between very distant galaxies due to space itself expanding |
| **Hubble's Law** | The farther away a galaxy is, the faster it appears to move away from us |
| **Dark Energy** | The leading (but still mysterious) explanation for why expansion is **accelerating** |

> [!quote]
> **The universe is undergoing cosmic expansion.** What is causing it? We don't know for sure — dark energy is the best current explanation, but its true nature remains one of the biggest unsolved problems in physics.
